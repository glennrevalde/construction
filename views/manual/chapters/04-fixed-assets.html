The Fixed Assets module keeps the register of everything the company owns and uses for more than a year: buildings, machines, vehicles, computers, furniture. It records how each asset was bought, charges depreciation every month, removes the asset from the books when it is sold or scrapped, and keeps the history of every repair and every preventive maintenance task. All of it posts to the general ledger through the same engine as the rest of GSMaRT Construction System, so the balance sheet always agrees with the register.

How the Fixed Assets module works

Before you enter the first asset, set up these lists once, in this order:

  1. Chart of Accounts. Every category needs three accounts: the asset account (for example 1530 Transportation Equipment), its accumulated depreciation account (1535 Accumulated Depreciation - Transportation) and a depreciation expense account (6090 Depreciation Expense). See Chart of Accounts.
  2. Module Default Accounts. The account Gain / Loss on Disposal of Assets (4300 Gain (Loss) on Disposal of Assets in the demo) must be set, or disposals stop with a message. See Module Default Accounts.
  3. Asset Categories. A category groups similar assets and decides the three accounts they post to, plus the method and useful life that new assets start with.
  4. Asset Locations. Where the assets are kept. Locations let you list, report and depreciate one branch or building at a time.

Then the day-to-day work is:

  • Add each asset in the Fixed Asset Register. The same form can record the purchase (a check, a bill, or an opening balance).
  • At each month end, post depreciation in Run Depreciation.
  • Record repairs and servicing in Asset Maintenance, and schedule recurring work (change oil, registration renewal, cleaning) in Preventive Maintenance.
  • When an asset is sold, scrapped or donated, record it in Dispose of Assets.

The depreciation methods

Each asset uses one of four methods. Depreciation is always charged by month. For the three time-based methods, the program works out the charge for each asset year (the twelve months starting with the month the asset was acquired) and charges one-twelfth of it every month; an asset acquired on any day of July is depreciated for the whole of July. Units of production charges each month for the units used in it. Net book value (cost less accumulated depreciation) never goes below the salvage value.

Method How the charge is worked out Demo example
Straight-line (Cost less salvage) spread evenly over the useful life in months FA-00001, ₱7,500.00 a month
Declining balance Net book value times (multiplier ÷ life); switches to straight-line when that gives more FA-00002, ₱40,000.00 a month in year 1
Sum-of-the-years'-digits (Cost less salvage) times remaining years ÷ sum of the years FA-00003, ₱2,500.00 a month in year 1
Units of production (Cost less salvage) ÷ estimated total output, times the units used in the month FA-00004, ₱1.50 per unit
Example

The Packaging Machine PM-500 cost ₱500,000.00, has a salvage value of ₱50,000.00 and a useful life of 5 years, straight-line. It depreciates ₱450,000.00 over 60 months: ₱90,000.00 a year, ₱7,500.00 a month. The Delivery Van (L300) cost ₱1,200,000.00 with a salvage value of ₱200,000.00 over 5 years, declining balance with a multiplier of 2: the rate is 2 ÷ 5 = 40%, so year 1 is ₱480,000.00, or ₱40,000.00 a month.

For declining balance and sum-of-the-years'-digits the life is counted in whole years. In the last month of a time-based life the program charges whatever is left down to the salvage value, so rounding never leaves a few centavos behind.

Asset statuses

  • Active: in service and still depreciating.
  • Fully Depreciated: the net book value has reached the salvage value. The asset stays in service (it still appears under In service) but no more depreciation is charged.
  • Disposed: sold, scrapped or donated. It no longer depreciates and is left out of the lists of assets in service.

The Record the purchase box has four choices:

  • Paid now (Write Checks): the program writes a check from the chosen bank account.
  • On account (Enter Bills): the program enters a bill for the vendor, with the vendor's terms and due date. You pay it later in Pay Bills.
  • Existing asset (opening balance): for an asset the company already owned before it started using GSMaRT. The program writes an opening balance entry.

Step by step: add an asset bought by check

  1. Go to Fixed Assets > Fixed Asset Register and click New asset.
  2. Type the Description, for example Packaging Machine PM-500.
  3. Choose the Category, for example Machinery and Equipment. The method and life fill in from the category.
  4. Type the Serial / plate no. and choose the Location, Site, Default class and Vendor.
  5. Enter the Acquired date and the Cost.
  6. Check the Method, Useful life (years) and Salvage (residual) value, and change them if this asset is different.
  7. Under How was the asset acquired?, leave Record the purchase on Paid now (Write Checks) and choose the Bank account.
  8. Tick Cost is net of 12% input VAT (VAT added) if the supplier charged VAT.
  9. Click Save asset (or Save asset & New to enter the next asset). The program gives the asset its number and opens it.

Step by step: add an asset bought on account

  1. Follow steps 1 to 6 above, and make sure the Vendor is chosen.
  2. Set Record the purchase to On account (Enter Bills).
  3. Type the Vendor's invoice no. and tick the VAT box if it applies.
  4. Click Save asset. The bill appears in Vendors > Enter Bills and in Pay Bills, due according to the vendor's payment terms.

Step by step: add an asset the company already owned

  1. Follow steps 1 to 6 above. Enter the original Acquired date and the original Cost.
  2. Set Record the purchase to Existing asset (opening balance).
  3. Enter the Accumulated depreciation to date and the Depreciation recorded through date, for example the last day of the month before you started using the program.
  4. Click Save asset. The months up to the Depreciation recorded through date are treated as already depreciated; Run Depreciation continues from the month after.
Tip

To load many assets at once, use Import from CSV. Imported assets are added without an accounting entry unless you ask for an opening balance entry for them.

Viewing an asset

Click an asset number to open it. The left card shows the asset's details, the accumulated depreciation, the net book value and the status. (source) beside the acquisition date opens the check, bill or opening balance that recorded the purchase. On the right, the Annual schedule shows how much each asset year will depreciate (for time-based methods), and Depreciation posted lists every depreciation entry with its period, entry number, units and amount. An opening balance row appears first when the asset was entered with accumulated depreciation.

The buttons at the top:

  • Edit opens the asset form.
  • Dispose opens Dispose of Assets with this asset chosen. Hidden for disposed assets.
  • Compare methods opens the Depreciation Schedule by Asset report for this asset.
  • Back returns to the register.

For a declining balance asset the Method line also shows the rate, for example "2× straight-line rate = 40.00%". For a units-of-production asset it shows the estimated total output and the rate per unit. When the estimates were changed after depreciation started, a Change in estimate line shows from when, the net book value and the number of months the new basis uses.

Step by step: record the usage of a units-of-production asset

A units-of-production asset is depreciated only for months with a usage figure. You can enter it on the asset or directly on Run Depreciation.

  1. Open the asset from the register.
  2. In the Record usage (meter reading / production count) card, enter the Year, the Month and the Units used in that month (not the meter reading itself).
  3. Click Save. The last 24 months entered are listed under the button. Entering a month again replaces its figure.
Note

The usage for depreciation is separate from the odometer or running hours kept for maintenance. The meter reading on a maintenance record or a preventive maintenance task does not feed depreciation.

Editing an asset

Open the asset and click Edit. What you may change depends on whether depreciation has already been posted.

Before any depreciation is posted you may change everything, including the Acquired date and the Cost. When the purchase was recorded by this form (a check, a bill or an opening balance), the program corrects that transaction too, so the ledger stays in step with the asset. A note under the fields links to that transaction.

After depreciation is posted (or when the asset has opening accumulated depreciation):

  • The Acquired date and the Cost are locked. To correct them, void the depreciation entries first in Run Depreciation, then edit the asset.
  • The Category cannot change.
  • A change to the method, the useful life, the salvage value, the multiplier or the estimated total output is a change in estimate. A Changes apply from (change in estimate) date appears. The program leaves past depreciation as it is and spreads the remaining net book value over the rest of the new useful life, starting with the later of the month you enter and the month after the last depreciation posted.
Example

The Delivery Van was depreciated for July and August 2026 (₱80,000.00). In September you decide its life is 8 years, not 5. Edit the asset, change Useful life (years) to 8 and leave Changes apply from on today's date. From September 2026, the net book value of ₱1,120,000.00 less the ₱200,000.00 salvage value is depreciated, still by declining balance, over the remaining 94 months (96 months less the 2 already passed).

Good to know

  • Asset numbers come from the asset number series (FA-00001, FA-00002 ...). You cannot type your own number on the form.
  • Changing the location of an asset is a normal edit; there is no transfer entry, because the location does not change the accounts.
  • Messages you may see: "Enter the description and a useful life greater than zero.", "Choose the asset category.", "Enter the acquisition date and cost.", "Enter the estimated total units for units-of-production depreciation.", "Choose the bank account for the check.", "Choose the vendor for the bill.", "Enter the date depreciation was recorded through.", "The new salvage value is higher than the remaining net book value.", "The category cannot change after depreciation has been posted.", "The cost cannot be lower than the salvage value."
  • When you correct the cost of an asset whose purchase check or bill was changed by hand in the meantime, the program cannot find the asset line and says: "The purchase was recorded on ..., and its asset line no longer matches the old cost. Correct that transaction first, then the asset."
  • Paying by check is subject to your amount limit (Max check / bill payment / adjustment amount without approval in User Role Configuration). This form has no supervisor approval box. If the cost is above your limit and you do not have the Approve permission on Write Checks, the program stops with "Supervisor approval is required ...". Ask a user who may approve checks to add the asset, or write the check in Write Checks and add the asset with Already recorded elsewhere.
  • Voiding or deleting the purchase check or bill does not remove the asset; it only removes the (source) link. The register has no delete button, so an asset entered by mistake stays in the register: correct it instead, while no depreciation is posted.
  • Every addition and change is written to the audit trail, including changes in estimate with their effective date.

Run Depreciation

Run Depreciation posts the monthly depreciation of all assets in one journal entry. Do it once a month, after the month has ended and before you print the month's financial statements.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Run Depreciation. The Run depreciation button on the Fixed Asset Register and the Run Depreciation quick action on the Home page open it too.

The page opens on the previous month. In the demo, today is 09/27/2026, so it opens on August 2026, which has already been posted: every asset shows posted and the Total to post is 0.00.

Choose another month in Period to see what can be posted for it. Each asset shows the depreciation the program has worked out, ticked and ready to post.

Fields

Field What to enter / what it means
Period The month to depreciate. The entry is dated the last day of that month, as the note beside the filters says ("Entries are dated 08/31/2026.").
Location All locations, one location, or (No location). Only the assets of the chosen location are listed and posted.
Tick box Tick the assets to post. The box in the header ticks or clears all of them. Assets already posted for the period have no box, nor do time-based assets with nothing to post.
Asset, Category, Location, Default class, Method, Cost The asset's details. Default class is shown only when classes are in use.
Accumulated The accumulated depreciation before this run
Usage this month Units-of-production assets only: the units used in the period. Type it here or on the asset.
Depreciation The amount to post, or posted when the period was already done for this asset
Notes Warnings: "N earlier month(s) not yet depreciated", "enter usage first", "fully depreciated"
Memo (optional) The memo of the entry. Left blank, it reads "Depreciation for August 2026" (with " - location" when one location is chosen).
Class Choose a class to record the whole entry under it, or leave Class: each asset's own default class to record every asset under its default class. Shown when classes are set up.

Step by step: post the month's depreciation

  1. Go to Fixed Assets > Run Depreciation.
  2. Check the Period. Change it if you are not posting last month.
  3. For each units-of-production asset, type the units used in Usage this month. Click Save usage only if you want to keep the figures and post later.
  4. Read the Notes column. If an asset has earlier months not yet depreciated, post those months first (choose the earlier Period and post it).
  5. Untick any asset you do not want to depreciate this time.
  6. Type a Memo and choose a Class if needed.
  7. Click Post depreciation. The program posts one journal entry and shows "Depreciation of 57,650.00 posted for 4 asset(s): debit Depreciation Expense, credit Accumulated Depreciation."

Step by step: depreciate one location at a time

  1. Choose the Location at the top of the page, or click Run depreciation for this location in the Fixed Asset Register.
  2. Post as above. Only that location's assets are listed and posted, and the memo names the location.
  3. Choose the next location and post again.

Step by step: undo a depreciation run

  1. In Posted depreciation, click the entry number (for example JE-00002).
  2. Click Void (the entry stays, marked VOID, with no effect on the ledger) or Delete (the entry is removed; a full copy stays in the audit trail).
  3. Type the reason and confirm.

The assets in that run can then be depreciated again for that period. An asset that had become Fully Depreciated through that run goes back to Active.

The posted entry

Open a run from Posted depreciation to see it. The view shows the lines as they were posted and the General ledger entries created automatically. The buttons are Print, Void, Delete, History and Back to list. A depreciation entry cannot be edited; void it and post again instead.

Print shows the entry as a journal voucher titled DEPRECIATION, with Prepared by, Checked by and Approved by lines. Click Print on that page to send it to the printer.

What it records

The program adds up the depreciation of the selected assets by category (and by class) and writes one pair of lines for each: a debit to the category's depreciation expense account and a credit to its accumulated depreciation account. The August 2026 run of the demo:

Account Debit Credit
6090 Depreciation Expense ₱57,650.00
1555 Accumulated Depreciation - Computer and POS ₱2,500.00
1525 Accumulated Depreciation - Machinery ₱15,150.00
1535 Accumulated Depreciation - Transportation ₱40,000.00

The entry takes its number from the journal entry series (JE-00001, JE-00002 ...). For each asset the program also records the period, the units used and the amount, which appear on the asset under Depreciation posted. An asset whose net book value reaches its salvage value becomes Fully Depreciated.

Note

Categories that share the same expense and accumulated depreciation accounts are posted on one pair of lines. In the demo, Machinery and Equipment (₱7,500.00) and Production Machinery (usage-based) (₱7,650.00) both use 6090 and 1525, so the entry shows one pair of ₱15,150.00. The line description names only one of the two categories.

Good to know

  • A period can be posted only once per asset. To post it again, void or delete the run first.
  • The program never catches up skipped months by itself. If you skip a month, the Notes column warns you; choose the skipped month and post it.
  • A units-of-production asset without a usage figure for the period is skipped, with the warning "Skipped FA-00004 (enter the meter reading / production count first)".
  • A disposed asset is depreciated up to its disposal month and not after.
  • Messages: "Select the assets to depreciate." (nothing ticked), "Nothing to post for this period." (every ticked asset had nothing to charge).
  • The entry is dated the last day of the period. If that date is on or before the closing date, only users with the special permission to change closed periods can post it. See Company, Security & Closing Date.
  • Posting needs the create permission on this page; voiding and deleting need the delete permission; printing needs the print permission.

Dispose of Assets

Use Dispose of Assets when an asset leaves the company: it is sold, traded in, scrapped, donated or lost. The program brings the depreciation up to date, removes the asset's cost and accumulated depreciation from the books, records the money received, and posts the gain or loss.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Dispose of Assets. The Dispose button on an asset opens it with that asset chosen.

The page opens with an empty Asset box and today as the Disposal date. Below is the list of Disposed assets with their cost, proceeds and disposal entry.

When you choose an asset, its cost, accumulated depreciation, net book value and method appear, with the disposal form.

Step by step: dispose of an asset

  1. Go to Fixed Assets > Dispose of Assets (or open the asset and click Dispose).
  2. Choose the Asset and set the Disposal date. The page reloads with the asset's figures.
  3. If the yellow warning appears, leave Post them first (prorated to the disposal month) ticked so the depreciation is complete up to the disposal month.
  4. Type How / why, the Proceeds received and choose Received into.
  5. Click Record disposal and confirm "Dispose of this asset?".
  6. The program shows the Asset Disposal entry. The asset's status becomes Disposed.

Example

The Delivery Van (cost ₱1,200,000.00) is sold on 09/30/2026 for ₱1,050,000.00. With September depreciated, its accumulated depreciation is ₱120,000.00 and its net book value ₱1,080,000.00. The entry debits 1535 Accumulated Depreciation - Transportation ₱120,000.00, 1050 Undeposited Funds ₱1,050,000.00 and 4300 Gain (Loss) on Disposal of Assets ₱30,000.00 (the loss), and credits 1530 Transportation Equipment ₱1,200,000.00.

The disposal entry is recorded under the asset's site and numbered in the journal entry series. When the buyer paid straight into the bank, choose the bank account in Received into. Step by step: undo a disposal

  1. In Disposed assets, click the entry in the Entry column (or open it from the asset's status line).
  2. Click Void or Delete, type the reason and confirm.

The asset goes back to Active and its disposal date and proceeds are cleared. The catch-up depreciation entries are separate entries and stay posted; void them in Run Depreciation if they should go too.

Good to know

  • Messages: "Choose an asset in service.", "The disposal date is before the acquisition date.", "Enter how and why the asset was disposed of.", "Choose the account that received the proceeds." (proceeds entered without an account).
  • The gain or loss uses the default account Gain / Loss on Disposal of Assets. If it is not set, the program says so; set it in Module Default Accounts.
  • For a units-of-production asset, the catch-up only posts months that have a usage figure, and the estimate in the warning shows 0.00. Enter the usage for the missing months first (on the asset or in Run Depreciation).
  • Recording a disposal needs the create permission on this page.

Asset Maintenance

Asset Maintenance keeps the history of every repair, servicing, inspection and upgrade of an asset: the supplier, the spare parts (with part, serial and model numbers for warranty claims), the labor, pictures of the parts, and the cost. When you save a record, the program also records the payment in the books as a bill, a check or a cash voucher, so maintenance is entered only once.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Asset Maintenance. It is also a Home page quick action, and Maintenance records on Preventive Maintenance opens it.

The list shows the records of the last twelve months (and the next 30 days), void records left out.

Pictures of the materials and parts: choose one or more JPG, PNG, GIF or WEBP files, up to 8 MB each, and type a caption under each preview. Notes holds anything else. The box on the right totals Parts, Labor, Input VAT 12% and Total cost.

Step by step: record a repair paid on account

  1. Go to Fixed Assets > Asset Maintenance and click New maintenance record.
  2. Choose the Fixed asset, for example Delivery Van (L300) (FA-00002), and set the Date, Type and Status.
  3. Choose the Supplier of the parts / service and fill in Performed by, the job order number and the Meter reading.
  4. Describe the Problem reported / reason and the Maintenance description.
  5. Enter each spare part in Spare parts and materials.
  6. Enter each labor line in Labor, choosing By the hour or Bulk charge.
  7. Under How is it paid?, leave On account: enter a Bill (pay later in Pay Bills). Check the expense accounts and the VAT box.
  8. Add pictures if you have them.
  9. Click Save record. The message tells you which document was created, for example "Maintenance record MNT-00001 saved: Bill BILL-00003 for 12,500.00 was created."

Step by step: record work that is still in progress

  1. Enter the record as above, with Status set to In progress (post the cost later).
  2. Save it. No bill, check or cash voucher is made yet.
  3. When the work is done, open the record, click Edit, change Status to Completed, complete the parts and labor, and save. The payment is recorded now.

Viewing a record

Click the record number to open it. The top card shows the asset, date, type, status, supplier, performer, reference, location, meter reading, downtime, next service date, the preventive task done, the class and who entered it. Below are the spare parts (a Warranty until date still in the future shows in green), the labor, the pictures (click one to open it full size), the Cost box, and the Accounting box with the document that paid the cost and its ledger lines. When the cost is an unpaid bill, Pay this bill opens Pay Bills for the supplier.

The buttons are Edit (not for void records), Print, Void, Delete and Back to list.

Print gives the ASSET MAINTENANCE RECORD: the asset and its serial or plate number, the supplier and reference, the problem and the work done, the parts and labor tables, the total cost, and signature lines.

Step by step: change or cancel a record

  • To change it, click Edit, make the changes and save. The bill, check or cash voucher is updated to match. If you choose another How is it paid?, the old document is voided and a new one is created. Pictures are removed with the Remove link under each one on the edit form.
  • To void it, click Void, type the reason and click Void maintenance record. The record and its details stay, marked void, and its bill, check or cash voucher is voided.
  • To delete it, click Delete, type the reason and click Delete maintenance record. The record, its pictures and its accounting document are removed. A full copy is kept in the audit trail.

What it records

On a record with parts of ₱11,000.00 and labor of ₱1,500.00, paid on account with no VAT, the program enters a bill for the supplier:

Account Debit Credit
6030 Repairs and Maintenance (parts) ₱11,000.00
6030 Repairs and Maintenance (labor) ₱1,500.00
2000 Accounts Payable ₱12,500.00

With the VAT box ticked, 12% of the parts and labor is also debited to 1300 Input Tax. A check credits the bank account instead of Accounts Payable; a cash voucher credits the cash fund. The bill uses the supplier's terms, is dated the maintenance date and is recorded under the asset's site. It is a normal bill: it appears in Enter Bills, Pay Bills and the payables reports.

Good to know

  • Recording the payment needs permission on the page of the document: create (or edit) on Enter Bills for a bill, on Write Checks for a check, on Cash Voucher for a cash voucher. Without it the program says "Your role cannot create a Bill. Ask someone who can, or save the record with "No entry" and have accounting record the payment."
  • A check or cash voucher above your amount limit needs supervisor approval (the Supervisor approval box).
  • Messages: "Choose the fixed asset that was repaired or maintained.", "Describe the maintenance that was done, or enter the parts and labor.", "Enter the maintenance description: what the labor was for.", "Enter the parts or the labor cost, or choose "No entry" when there is nothing to pay.", "Choose the supplier: a bill is owed to a supplier.", "Choose the bank account the check is drawn on.", "Choose the cash account the voucher is paid from.", "The preventive maintenance task belongs to another asset.", "... is not a picture. Upload JPG, PNG, GIF or WEBP files."
  • A paid bill or a reconciled check blocks voiding or deleting the record until the payment is dealt with.
  • The Parts charged to and Labor charged to lists also offer Fixed Asset accounts. Charging a cost to an asset account does not change the asset's cost in the register, so the register and the ledger would no longer agree.

Preventive Maintenance

Preventive Maintenance keeps the recurring tasks that fall due by date, by meter reading, or both: vehicle registration renewal, insurance renewal, change oil, tune-up, cleaning of air-conditioning units, computers and solar panels, calibration, permits. A task that is due keeps reminding everyone who may open this page until someone acknowledges that it is complete. A repeating task then moves to its next due date.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Preventive Maintenance. It also opens from the bell in the top bar, from the Asset maintenance due card on the Home page, from the reminder pop-up, and from the Preventive maintenance button on Asset Maintenance.

The three cards at the top count the active tasks: Overdue (past their due date or meter), Due soon (inside their reminder period) and Upcoming (scheduled, not reminding yet). Click a card to see those tasks.

How a task becomes due

  • By date: a task starts reminding the chosen number of days before its due date (Due in 20 days) and becomes overdue the day after (3 days overdue).
  • By meter: a task starts reminding when the asset's current meter reading is within 10% of the meter interval of the due reading, and becomes overdue when the reading reaches it. For a change oil every 5,000 km due at 50,000 km, it reminds from 49,500 km.
  • A task with both a date and a meter takes whichever is more urgent.

The tabs

Every tab can be narrowed with the Asset, Location and Kind of task filters.

Reminding now lists the overdue and due tasks, the most urgent first. Overdue rows have a red edge and due rows an orange edge.

Upcoming lists the active tasks that are not reminding yet.

All tasks lists every task, including stopped and finished ones.

Completed is the log of every task acknowledged as complete: Completed (date), Asset, Task, Was due (in red when done late), Meter, Remarks, Acknowledged by and Record (the maintenance record, when the task was done through one). The Kind of task filter does not apply to this tab.

The task lists show Asset (with its description and location), Task (with its notes), Due (the date, and the due meter with the current reading), State, Repeats, Last done and Responsible, followed by the Completed button, the edit button (pencil) and the delete button (trash).

Adding a task

Click New task. The form opens above the tabs.

Step by step: add a recurring task

  1. Click New task.
  2. Choose the Fixed asset and the Kind of task, and check the Task name.
  3. Enter the Next due date and Remind (days before).
  4. Leave Repeats ticked and enter Every (for example 12 month(s)), or a meter interval with the Next due at meter reading, or both.
  5. Enter the current meter reading if the task goes by meter, the Person responsible and any Notes.
  6. Click Save task (or Save task & New). The message tells you when it starts reminding, for example "Task saved. It starts reminding 14 day(s) before 10/17/2026."

To change a task, click its pencil button. The form opens as Edit task.

Step by step: acknowledge a task as complete

  1. Click Completed on the task's row. The dialog Acknowledge the task as complete opens.
  2. Enter the Date completed (it cannot be in the future).
  3. Enter the Meter reading. It is required when the task repeats by meter.
  4. Type the Remarks (official receipt number, who did it, findings).
  5. Tick Then record the parts, labor and payment in Asset Maintenance if there is a cost to record.
  6. Click Task is complete.

The task is written to the Completed log and its reminder stops. A repeating task moves to its next due date: counted from the due date when the work was done on time or early, from the completion date when it was late. The next due meter is the reading entered plus the meter interval. A one-time task is finished. If you ticked the last box, a new maintenance record opens for the asset.

Tip

When the work has a cost, you can instead create the maintenance record first and choose the task in Preventive maintenance task this completes. Saving the record as Completed acknowledges the task in one step and links the record in the log.

The reminders

  • The bell in the top bar shows the number of tasks due or overdue. Click it to open this page on Reminding now. With nothing due, the bell is quiet.
  • The pop-up "Maintenance due: N tasks" appears after you log in, once a day, and again whenever the list of due tasks changes. Later closes it; Open Preventive Maintenance opens this page. It does not appear on this page itself.
  • The Home page card "Asset maintenance due" lists up to 12 due tasks with their state and date, and an Open button.

The reminders are shown to every user who may open Preventive Maintenance, and they stay until the task is acknowledged as complete. Disposed assets never remind.

Good to know

  • Delete (trash button) removes a task that was never completed. A task with completed entries is stopped instead, and its log stays: "The task was stopped. Its 1 completed entries stay in the log."
  • Messages: "Choose the fixed asset.", "Enter when the task is next due: a date, a meter reading, or both.", "Enter the meter reading at which the task is next due.", "Enter the meter reading: this task repeats by km.", "The completion date cannot be in the future."
  • If a task should no longer repeat by meter, clear or every (meter: km, hours) before unticking Repeats; the meter interval is kept otherwise.
  • Adding tasks needs the create permission, editing and acknowledging need the edit permission, deleting needs the delete permission.
  • The Upcoming Preventive Maintenance and Completed Preventive Maintenance reports print these lists.

Asset Categories

Asset categories group similar assets and decide which accounts they post to. The category also gives new assets their default depreciation method and useful life.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Asset Categories.

The list on the left shows each category with its three account codes (Asset / Accum. / Expense), Default method, default Life and the number of Assets in it. Inactive categories are shown in grey. The form on the right adds a new category.

Fields

Field What to enter / what it means
Name The category name, for example Transportation Equipment. Required.
Asset account The Fixed Asset account the cost is debited to, for example 1530 Transportation Equipment
Accumulated depreciation account The Fixed Asset account credited by depreciation, for example 1535 Accumulated Depreciation - Transportation
Depreciation expense account The expense account debited by depreciation, for example 6090 Depreciation Expense. Expense, Other Expense and Cost of Goods Sold accounts are offered.
Default method The method a new asset in this category starts with
Default life (years) The useful life a new asset starts with (at least 0.5)
Active Untick it to stop offering the category on the asset form. Its assets are not affected.

Step by step: add or change a category

  1. Go to Fixed Assets > Asset Categories.
  2. To add one, fill in the New category form. To change one, click Edit on its row; the form becomes Edit category.
  3. Enter the Name and choose the three accounts.
  4. Choose the Default method and Default life (years).
  5. Click Save category (or Save category & New). Cancel leaves the edit form.

Good to know

  • All three accounts are required: "Enter the name and all three accounts."
  • Create the accounts first in the Chart of Accounts. Asset and accumulated depreciation accounts must be of the type Fixed Asset to appear in the lists.
  • The accounts are read from the category each time depreciation is run or an asset is disposed of. Changing the accounts of a category that already has assets sends their later depreciation and disposals to the new accounts, while their cost and past depreciation stay in the old ones. Prefer a new category, or move the balances with a journal entry.
  • The default method and life only fill in the form of a new asset; changing them does not change existing assets.
  • Categories cannot be deleted. Make an unused one inactive.

Fixed asset reports

The fixed asset reports are under Fixed Assets > Reports in the sidebar and in the Report Center. Their common tools (Refresh, Columns, Print / PDF, Export to spreadsheet, Email link, Modify Report, Memorize, and clicking a figure to see the detail) are explained in Working with reports. Most of them have a Location filter with (No location) for assets without one.

Fixed Asset Register Report

The register of assets as of a date, with their cost, salvage value, accumulated depreciation and net book value. Use it at month end or year end to support the fixed asset accounts on the balance sheet.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Reports > Fixed Asset Register Report.

The filters: As of, Asset category and Location.

The columns: Asset no. (click to open the asset), Description, Category, Location, Acquired, Method, Life (yrs), Cost, Salvage, Accum. dep. and Net book value, with a TOTAL row. The report lists the assets acquired on or before the date and not disposed of by then. Accumulated depreciation includes opening accumulated depreciation and the depreciation posted for months up to the as-of month.

Tip

Run it as of the last day of a month after posting that month's depreciation. The Cost total should equal the balance of the asset accounts and the Accum. dep. total the balance of the accumulated depreciation accounts on the balance sheet.

Depreciation Schedule by Asset

Compares straight-line, double-declining balance and sum-of-the-years'-digits year by year for one asset, or for any figures you type in. Use it to choose a method before adding an asset, or to explain an asset's depreciation.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Reports > Depreciation Schedule by Asset, or Compare methods on an asset.

The filters: Asset, or with Asset on All, the Cost, Salvage and Life (years) of an example asset. Left blank, the example uses a cost of ₱500,000.00, a salvage value of ₱50,000.00 and 5 years.

The columns: Year, the yearly depreciation by Straight-line, Double-declining balance and Sum-of-the-years'-digits, and the net book value at the end of each year under each method (NBV (SL), NBV (DDB), NBV (SYD)). The note above the table gives the depreciable cost, the double-declining rate and, for a units-of-production asset, the rate per unit.

Note

The comparison always uses a multiplier of 2 for declining balance and whole years for the life, whatever the asset's own settings. All three methods depreciate the same total; only the timing differs.

Depreciation by Category

The depreciation posted in a period, by category and asset. Use it to check the depreciation expense of the month or year and to see which assets it came from.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Reports > Depreciation by Category.

The filters: Dates (this fiscal year by default), From, To, Asset category and Location.

The columns: Category / asset (grouped by category with a total for each), Method, Units used (units-of-production assets) and Depreciation. Only depreciation entries dated in the period and not voided are counted; opening accumulated depreciation is not.

Additions and Disposals

The assets acquired and the assets disposed of in a period. Use it for the fixed asset movement schedule of the financial statements.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Reports > Additions and Disposals.

The filters: Dates, From, To and Location.

The report can also be narrowed by Asset category (see Filters on related records).

The columns: Two sections. Additions lists each asset acquired in the period with its Category, Date and Cost, and Total additions. Disposals lists each asset disposed of in the period with its Cost, Accum. dep., Proceeds and Gain / (loss), and Total disposals.

Maintenance History

Every maintenance record in a period with its cost and the document that paid it. Use it to review what was spent on repairs and servicing.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Reports > Maintenance History.

The filters: Dates, From, To, Vendor, Class, Asset, Location and Type (repair, preventive maintenance, inspection, upgrade).

The columns: Date, Record (click to open it), Asset, Location, Type, Work done, Supplier, Reference, Parts, Labor, VAT, Total and Paid through. Void records are left out.

Maintenance Cost by Asset

The maintenance cost of each asset in a period, most expensive first, compared with what the asset cost. An asset whose maintenance keeps climbing is a candidate for replacement.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Reports > Maintenance Cost by Asset.

The filters: Dates, From, To, Class, Asset category, Location and Type.

The columns: Asset (click to open its maintenance records), Category, Location, Jobs (number of records), Repairs (records of the type Repair), Parts, Labor, Total cost (before VAT), % of cost (the period's maintenance as a percentage of the asset's cost), Downtime (days) and Last job.

Spare Parts Used

Every spare part recorded on maintenance records in a period, with its identifying numbers, supplier and warranty. Use it to follow up warranty claims and to see which parts are replaced often.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Reports > Spare Parts Used.

The filters: Dates, From, To, Vendor, Asset and Location.

The columns: Date, Record, Asset, Part no., Description, Brand, Serial no., Model no., Size, Qty, Unit, Unit cost, Amount, Supplier and Warranty until.

Tip

To find one part across all records, use the Search box of the Asset Maintenance list: it searches part numbers, descriptions, serials and models.

Maintenance Labor

Every labor line of the maintenance records in a period: the work, who did it, the man-hours and the charge.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Reports > Maintenance Labor.

The filters: Dates, From, To, Vendor, Asset and Location.

The columns: Date, Record, Asset, Work done, Worker / mechanic (the record's Performed by when no worker was typed on the line), Charge (By the hour or Bulk charge), Man-hours, Rate, Amount and Supplier. The total row adds up the man-hours (bulk charges have none) and the amounts.

Upcoming Preventive Maintenance

The preventive maintenance tasks that are overdue, due now, or coming up. Print it for the person responsible, or to plan the month's servicing.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Reports > Upcoming Preventive Maintenance, or Upcoming report on Preventive Maintenance.

The filters: Asset, Location, Kind of task, and Show: Overdue and due now, Due within 30 days, Due within 90 days, Due within a year or Every active task. Overdue and due tasks are always included.

The columns: Asset, Location, Task (click it to open the asset's tasks), Kind, Due date, Due at meter, State, Repeats, Last done, Responsible and Notes. Overdue tasks are shown in red. The note above the table counts the overdue, due and upcoming tasks.

Note

When the report first opens, the Show box displays Overdue and due now, but the report lists the tasks due within 90 days. Choose the option you want and click Refresh.

Completed Preventive Maintenance

The tasks acknowledged as complete in a period, and whether they were done on time.

Where to find it: Fixed Assets > Reports > Completed Preventive Maintenance.

The filters: Dates, From, To, Asset and Location.

The columns: Completed, Asset, Location, Task, Was due, Timing ("5 day(s) late", "2 day(s) early" or "on the due date"), Meter, Remarks, Acknowledged by, Maintenance record (click to open it) and Cost (the total of that record).

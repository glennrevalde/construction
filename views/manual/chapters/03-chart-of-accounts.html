The Financial Set-up & Reports module (called Chart of Accounts before version 29) holds the accounts that every transaction posts to, the lists the other modules share, the journal entries you make by hand, and the financial statements. Most entries reach the general ledger on their own: an invoice, a bill, a check or a POS sale writes its ledger lines the moment it is saved. This chapter explains how to set the accounts up, how to record what the modules cannot post for you, and how to read the ledger and the statements.

In the sidebar the module has four groups:

  • Lists: Chart of Accounts, Class List, Employees & Other Names, Memorized Transactions.
  • Setup: Set Up Budgets, Module Default Accounts, Terms, Tax Codes & Other Lists.
  • Transactions: Make Journal Entries.
  • Reports: the trial balances, balance sheets, income statements, the comparative income statements and balance sheets (by month, quarter, year and your own ranges or dates), cash flow, ledgers and journal. Click Reports to open the list. The Report Center lists them in its Financial Reports card.
Note

You see only the pages and buttons your role allows (see Page Access Configuration). Adding, changing or deleting accounts and changing the module default accounts also needs the special permission Can change the Chart of Accounts and default accounts, set in User Role Configuration.

Chart of Accounts

The Chart of Accounts is the list of every account the books use: cash and bank accounts, receivables, inventory, payables, equity, income and expenses. Here you add accounts, group them under parent accounts, mark the ones that keep a subsidiary ledger, and open the ledger or the register of any account.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Chart of Accounts

The accounts are shown in five cards, Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Incomes and Expenses, each sorted by code. A sub-account is indented under its parent with an arrow, for example 1010 Cash in Bank - BDO Checking under 1000 Cash. Accounts that the modules need, such as 1100 Accounts Receivable, 1200 Inventory and 2000 Accounts Payable, carry a grey system tag. Tick Include inactive accounts above the list to show the accounts you have made inactive; they appear greyed out.

The columns

Column What it shows
Code The account code. The list is sorted by it.
Name The account name. Click it to open the General Ledger of this account.
Type The account type (see the table below).
Normal Debit or Credit: the side that increases the account.
With SL Yes when the account keeps a subsidiary ledger.
Balance The balance of the account on its normal side, for all dates. A negative balance is shown in brackets.
Links Edit, Register and Delete, for the users allowed to use them.

The balance of a parent account is only what was posted to the parent itself; the balances of its sub-accounts are not added to it. In the demo company, 1000 Cash shows 0.00 while its sub-account 1010 Cash in Bank - BDO Checking shows 815,734.14.

Bank balances show as ••• to a user who has neither the special permission View bank balances outside Banking nor access to Use Register.

Some types also change what the forms allow:

  • Bank accounts are the ones offered on Write Checks, Make Deposits, the cash account of a Cash Voucher and Reconcile. Create cash funds such as a petty cash fund or a revolving fund as Bank accounts too, so they can be paid out of and reconciled.
  • Credit Card accounts can be chosen on Write Checks and Transfer Funds.
  • Other Current Asset accounts can be chosen in Transfer Funds and as the Deposit to account of a sales receipt.
  • A journal entry line to an Accounts Receivable account needs a customer, and a line to an Accounts Payable account needs a vendor.

Fields

Step by step

To add an account:

  1. Open Financial Set-up & Reports > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Click New account.
  3. Choose the Account type.
  4. Type the Code and the Name.
  5. If the account belongs under another one, choose it in Sub-account of.
  6. Leave Normal balance at From type unless this is a contra account.
  7. Tick With SL if you want the account to keep a subsidiary ledger.
  8. For a bank account, type the Bank account no.
  9. Click Save account, or Save account & New to save it and start another one straight away.

To change an account:

  1. Click Edit on the account's line.
  2. Change what you need. The title of the form shows the code, for example "Edit account 1000".
  3. Click Save account.

To make an account inactive:

  1. Click Edit on the account's line.
  2. Untick Active.
  3. Click Save account.

To delete an account:

  1. Find the account. A Delete link appears only on an account that is not a system account and whose balance is zero.
  2. Click Delete and confirm "Delete this account?".

To open an account's register: click Register on its line. The link appears for Bank, Credit Card, Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable, Other Current Asset and Other Current Liability accounts. See Use Register.

To export the list: click Export. The file chart_of_accounts.csv holds the code, name, type, category, normal balance, parent, With SL, balance and active flag of every account shown. Export needs the print/export permission on this page.

Buttons

  • New account: opens a blank account form. Shown only to users with the special permission to change the Chart of Accounts.
  • Export: downloads the list as a spreadsheet file.
  • Module default accounts: opens Module Default Accounts.

With SL: keeping a subsidiary ledger

An account marked With SL keeps a listing of the names that transacted it, like a subsidiary ledger of advances to employees, deposits from customers or loans from officers. While the box is ticked:

  • every journal entry, cash voucher and check that posts to the account must name a customer, supplier, employee or other name. On a journal entry or cash voucher the name is chosen on the line; on a check, the payee of the check counts for every line.
  • saving without a name is refused with a message that names the account, for example "The account 1400 - Prepaid Expenses is marked "With SL", so the line posting to it needs a name: choose a customer, supplier, employee or other name."
  • the Subsidiary Ledger and the Schedule of Accounts list the account and its names.

The rule applies to entries saved after the box is ticked. Entries made earlier keep whatever name they had.

What it records

Adding or changing an account posts nothing. Every change is written to the audit trail.

Good to know

  • "Enter the account code and name." Both are required.
  • "Another account uses this code." Choose a different code.
  • "A sub-account must be under a parent account of the same category." Choose a parent of the same category, or change the type.
  • "System accounts used by the modules cannot be made inactive." Accounts with the system tag must stay active.
  • "This account has transactions; it cannot be moved to a different category." You can change the type within the same category (for example from Expense to Other Expense), but not from an expense to an asset once entries have been posted.
  • "This account is in use. Make it inactive instead of deleting it." An account cannot be deleted while it has ledger entries or transaction lines, is a module default account, has sub-accounts, or is used on an item. Make it inactive instead.
  • Without the special permission to change the Chart of Accounts, the list is read-only and the New account, Edit and Delete links do not appear.

Class List

Classes split the books your own way: by branch, department, project or product line. Every transaction form has a Class box, the class is copied to every ledger line the transaction posts, and the financial reports can be filtered by class or shown with one column per class. You name the classes yourself and can rename them at any time.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Class List

The columns

Column What it shows
Class name The name shown on forms and reports.
Description Your note about the class.
Order The position of the class in drop-down lists (lowest first).
Entries How many transactions carry this class.
Net income (FY) Income less expenses posted with this class in the current fiscal year. Shown only to users who may open Income Statement by Class.
Active Yes or No.

Fields

Field What to enter / what it means
Class name Up to 80 characters, for example Main Branch, Piggery or Meat Shop. Two classes cannot share a name.
Order in lists A whole number. Classes are listed from the lowest number up, then by name.
Description An optional note, up to 255 characters.
Active (offered on transaction forms) Untick to stop offering the class on new transactions. It stays on the transactions that already have it and in the report filters.

Step by step

To add a class:

  1. Open Financial Set-up & Reports > Class List.
  2. In the New class panel, type the Class name.
  3. Type the Order in lists and a Description if you want them.
  4. Click Save class, or Save class & New to add another one straight away.

To rename or change a class:

  1. Click Edit on the class's line. The panel becomes Edit class.
  2. Change the name, order, description or Active box.
  3. Click Save class. Click Cancel to go back to a blank form without saving.

To delete a class: click Delete on its line and confirm. The link appears only on a class that no transaction carries.

Buttons

  • Income Statement by Class and Balance Sheet by Class open the two reports that show one column per class.

What it records

Nothing is posted. The class you choose on a transaction is copied to its ledger lines when the transaction is saved.

Good to know

  • "Renaming a class changes its name everywhere, including past transactions and reports." Reports always show the current name.
  • "Another class already has this name." Choose a different name.
  • "Cebu Store is already used on transactions, so it cannot be deleted. Make it inactive instead." Any use counts, including a line of a journal entry or a POS terminal's default class.
  • When you save a transaction with the Class box empty, the program asks first: No class selected, "This transaction has no class. It will be saved as Unclassified and will not appear under any class on the class reports." Click Choose a class to go back to the box, or Continue saving. The question is not asked while no class exists yet.
  • On journal entries and cash vouchers each line can carry its own class. The class at the top of the form fills the class of every line that has an account.

Memorized Transactions

A memorized transaction is a saved copy of a transaction you enter again and again: the monthly rent check, a standing invoice, the payroll journal entry. It keeps the names, accounts, items and amounts; the date and the document number are given each time it is entered. You can enter it from the list whenever you need it, get a reminder when it is due, or let the program enter it on its due date.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Memorized Transactions. The Home page also shows Memorized transactions due with an Open button when a reminder is due (see Home Dashboard).

The columns

Column What it shows
Name and type The name you gave it, with its type (Invoice, Bill, Check, Journal Entry, Inventory Adjustment and so on) and its notes underneath.
Name on the transaction The customer, supplier or payee of the saved copy.
Amount The total of the saved copy.
Schedule How it is entered: Automatically, Reminder, When needed or, for an adjustment or a batch of invoices, Loaded when needed (the full wording shows when you point at it), with the frequency under it, for example "Every month" or "Every 2 weeks".
Next due The next due date, with Due or Overdue under it when a reminder is due.
Entered How many times it was entered, for example "3 of 12 times" when a number is set, with the date of the last entry under it.

Lines with a reminder due are marked with a coloured edge. Inactive ones are greyed out. When you use groups, each group has its own heading line.

Memorizing a transaction

You add to this list in two ways, for users who may create memorized transactions:

  • From the form, before saving: Memorize Transaction beside the save buttons of every transaction form (see Memorize Transaction and Load Memorized Transaction), and Memorize Adjustment on Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand. Item receipts, batches of invoices and inventory adjustments are memorized this way.
  • From a saved transaction: the Memorize button on the saved (not voided) invoices, sales receipts, estimates, sales orders, statement charges, credit memos, bills, supplier debit memos, purchase orders, checks, journal entries and cash vouchers.

Load Memorized Transaction on a new form fills the form from one of them. On this list, the Open button (open on the form) does the same: it opens a new form of its kind filled from it.

Field What to enter / what it means
Name it Starts as the name on the transaction and its type, for example "Accounting Partners & Co. Bill". Required.
Group (optional) A heading to group related entries, for example "Month-end".
How it is entered Enter it automatically, Remind me when it is due or Don't remind me (I enter it from the list).
Every A number and a unit: Day(s), Week(s), Month(s), Quarter(s) or Year(s).
Next due The date of the next entry. It starts one month after the transaction's date.
Remind (days before) How many days before the due date the reminder starts (0 to 90).
Number remaining (blank = no limit) How many more times it should be entered, for example 11 for the rest of a one-year lease.
Notes Shown under the name on the list.

The three ways of entering:

  • Enter it automatically: the program records the entry on its due date, the first time anyone opens the system that day, and shows "Memorized transaction "..." was entered automatically for 09/03/2026." If several due dates were missed, it catches up one date at a time, each entry with its own date.
  • Remind me when it is due: from the set number of days before the due date, the line shows Due (or Overdue once the date has passed) and the Home page lists it. You enter it with Enter now.
  • Don't remind me (I enter it from the list): it waits on the list until you enter it.

Step by step

To memorize a transaction:

  1. Open the saved transaction, for example a bill from Accounting Partners & Co.
  2. Click Memorize.
  3. Check Name it and choose a Group if you use groups.
  4. Choose How it is entered.
  5. Set Every, for example 1 Month(s), and the Next due date.
  6. Set Remind (days before) and Number remaining if needed.
  7. Click Memorize. The message "Memorized as "...". It is on the Memorized Transactions list." confirms it.

To enter a memorized transaction now:

  1. Open Financial Set-up & Reports > Memorized Transactions.
  2. Click Enter now on its line. The dialog Enter a memorized transaction opens.
  3. Check the Date of the entry. It starts at the next due date.
  4. Leave Move the schedule on to the next due date ticked if this entry is the one that was due. Untick it for an extra entry that should not move the schedule.
  5. Click Enter it. The document is recorded straight away and opens so you can check it.

To change the schedule:

  1. Click the pencil button on the line.
  2. In the Edit form at the top, change the Name, Group (optional), How it is entered, Every, Next due, Remind (days before), Number remaining (blank = no limit), Stop after, Active or Notes.
  3. Click Save.

To take one off the list: click the trash button and confirm "Take "..." off the memorized list? The transactions already entered from it are not touched."

Buttons

  • New journal entry opens a blank Make Journal Entries form.
  • Enter now is shown on active lines to users who may create memorized transactions. It is green when the line is due.
  • Open opens a new form filled from the line, to change and save. A batch of invoices and an inventory adjustment are always used this way (their figures are revised each time), so they have no Enter now; their button is green when they are due.

What it records

Entering a memorized transaction saves an ordinary transaction of its type, with a new number and the date you chose, and posts it exactly as if you had typed it. An invoice or bill gets its due date from its terms. After the entry, Last entered, Entered and Number remaining are updated and Next due moves on by the frequency. Each entry is noted in the audit trail.

Good to know

  • The saved copy does not change when you edit the original transaction later. To change the amounts, memorize the corrected transaction again and delete the old line.
  • When Number remaining reaches zero, or the next due date falls after Stop after, the memorized transaction becomes inactive and is no longer entered or reminded.
  • An automatic entry that cannot be recorded (for example because its date is on or before the closing date, or a default account is missing) is switched to Remind me when it is due and the reason is written to the audit trail. Enter it by hand once the problem is fixed.
  • Opening balance entries and voided transactions cannot be memorized.
  • The transactions entered from the list are ordinary transactions: change, void or delete them on their own pages.

Set Up Budgets

A budget holds the amounts you plan for each account, month by month, for one fiscal year. A budget is for the income statement or for the balance sheet, and can be limited to one class. The Income Statement vs Budget and Balance Sheet vs Budget reports put the actual figures beside it and show the difference.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Set Up Budgets. The same page, for sales forecasts per customer, is Customers > Set Up Forecast (see Set Up Forecast).

The columns

Column What it shows
Name The budget's name. Click it to enter the amounts.
What it budgets Income statement (profit and loss) or Balance sheet.
Fiscal year The year, or the months it covers when the fiscal year does not start in January.
Class The class the budget is for, if any.
Yearly total The sum of all its monthly amounts.
Notes Your notes.

Each line has an Amounts button and a delete (trash) button.

Fields

Field What to enter / what it means
Name For example "Budget 2027". Required.
What it budgets Income statement (profit and loss) lists the Income, Other Income, Cost of Goods Sold, Expense and Other Expense accounts. Balance sheet lists the asset, liability and equity accounts.
Fiscal year The year the budget is for. The months follow the fiscal year set in Company, Security & Closing Date; the form says in which month it starts.
Class (optional) Leave blank for the whole company, or choose a class to budget one branch or department. The actual figures are then only those of that class.
Notes Optional.
Start from last year's actual figures Fills the grid with the actual figures of the year before, so you adjust them instead of typing everything.

The amounts grid

After Create, the budget opens in its amounts grid. Each active account of the budget's kind has a row, grouped by account type, with a box for each of the twelve months and a Year total. The Total row at the bottom adds up each month.

  • Fill a row: type an amount, click in any box of a row, then click Across. Every month of that row gets the amount.
  • Yearly total: type a yearly amount, click in a row, then click Spread. The amount is divided evenly over the twelve months; the last month takes the centavos left over.
  • Notes: change the budget's notes.

Enter income and expense amounts as positive figures. For a balance sheet budget, enter the balance you expect at the end of each month, also as positive figures for assets, liabilities and equity.

Step by step

To create a budget:

  1. Open Financial Set-up & Reports > Set Up Budgets.
  2. Click New budget.
  3. Type the Name, choose What it budgets and type the Fiscal year.
  4. Choose a Class if the budget is for one class.
  5. Tick Start from last year's actual figures if you want the grid filled for you.
  6. Click Create. The grid opens with the message "Budget 2027 created. Enter the monthly amounts below."
  7. Type or change the monthly amounts.
  8. Click Save the amounts. The message says how many monthly amounts were saved.

To change the amounts later: click the budget's name or Amounts, change the figures and click Save the amounts.

To compare with the actual figures: on the grid click Compare with actual, or open the report from Income statement vs budget on the list.

To delete a budget: click the trash button on its line and confirm "Delete "..." and its amounts?".

What it records

A budget posts nothing to the books. It is only compared with the ledger by the budget reports.

Good to know

  • "There is already a budget called "Budget 2027" for 2027." Two budgets of the same kind and year cannot share a name.
  • "Enter the fiscal year." The year must be between 2000 and 2100.
  • When Start from last year's actual figures is ticked, the copied figures are only shown on the grid ("Last year's actual figures were copied in. Change what you need and press Save."). They are kept only after you click Save the amounts.
  • Empty boxes and zeros are not saved.
  • The Compare with actual button opens the report on the newest budget of that kind. If you have several budgets, choose the one you want in the report's Budget box.

Make Journal Entries

A journal entry posts debits and credits directly to the accounts you choose. Routine activity posts automatically from invoices, bills, payments and the POS, so you use journal entries for what no other form records: opening balances, accruals, reclassifications, corrections and closing entries.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Make Journal Entries opens the list of journal entries. The Home page quick action Make Journal Entries opens a blank entry.

The list shows the journal entries and opening balance entries of a date range (from 90 days back to 30 days ahead when it opens). Use From, To, Status (Posted or Void), Class and Search (number, name or memo), then click Filter. Click a number to open the entry. Export downloads the list.

Fields

Field What to enter / what it means
Date The date the entry posts on.
Entry no. Leave blank to get the next number (the grey text shows it, for example JE-00005). Type your own number only if you need one.
Reference An outside reference, for example a document number.
Memo What the entry is for. It is used as the memo of every line that has none.
Class The class of the whole entry. It fills the class of every line.
Opening balance entry Tick for the entry that brings in your opening balances. It is then labelled Opening Balance instead of Journal Entry.
Reason for change Shown only when you edit a saved entry. Recorded in the audit trail.

The lines:

Column What to enter / what it means
Account Type part of the account name to find it, for example "rent" for 6010 Rent Expense.
Debit The debit amount of the line.
Credit The credit amount of the line. A line has a debit or a credit, not both.
Memo The description of the line.
Name A customer, supplier, employee or other name. Required on Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable and With SL accounts.
Class The class of the line. Change it only when this line belongs to another class.

Under the lines, Totals shows the debits and the credits, and Difference shows what is still out of balance. Add line adds a row; the red X removes one.

Step by step

To make a journal entry:

  1. Open Financial Set-up & Reports > Make Journal Entries and click New journal entry.
  2. Check the Date.
  3. Type a Reference and a Memo.
  4. Choose the Class if you use classes.
  5. On the first line, choose the Account and type the Debit.
  6. On the next line, choose the account and type the Credit.
  7. Add more lines if needed, until Difference shows 0.00.
  8. Choose a Name on every line that needs one.
  9. Click Save entry, or Save entry & New to start another one.
Example

To accrue September rent of ₱85,000.00 that will be billed in October, debit 6010 Rent Expense ₱85,000.00 and credit 2200 Accrued Expenses ₱85,000.00, with the memo "Accrued rent, September".

To enter opening balances:

  1. Start a new journal entry dated the day before you begin using the system.
  2. Tick Opening balance entry.
  3. Debit each asset account with its balance and credit each liability and equity account with its balance.
  4. Save when Difference is 0.00. In the demo company, OB-0001 debits 1010 Cash in Bank - BDO Checking ₱2,500,000.00 and the other cash accounts, and credits 3000 Owner's Capital ₱3,320,000.00.

To change a journal entry:

  1. Open the entry from the list.
  2. Click Edit.
  3. Change the lines or the header.
  4. Type a Reason for change.
  5. Click Save entry.

To print a journal entry: open it and click Print, then click Print above the page. See the print format below.

To void or delete a journal entry: open it, click Void or Delete, type the reason and confirm. Voiding keeps the entry with no effect on the ledger; deleting removes it, and a full copy stays in the audit trail.

To memorize a journal entry: open it and click Memorize (see Memorized Transactions).

The saved entry shows the date, number, status, memo, class and who entered it, the lines as you entered them, and General ledger entries created automatically with the total debits and credits. A ledger line that was cleared in a bank reconciliation is marked cleared.

Buttons

  • Edit, Print, Memorize, Void, Delete: see the procedures above. Each appears only when you have the permission.
  • History opens the Transaction History of the entry.
  • Back to list returns to the Journal Entries list.
  • Journal list (on the form) goes back to the list without saving.

The print format

The printed journal entry has the company name, address and TIN (and the logo, when the administrator has switched it on for this document), the title JOURNAL ENTRY or OPENING BALANCE, the number and the date. Under it are the memo, reference, site and class, then a table with Account, Description, Name, Class, Debit and Credit, the two totals, and the signature lines Prepared by (the user who entered it), Checked by and Approved by. A line without its own class prints the class of the entry.

What it records

Each line posts to its account exactly as entered: a debit line debits the account, a credit line credits it. The name and the class of the line go with it to the ledger, so a line to 1100 Accounts Receivable with the name ABC Trading Corp. changes that customer's balance. An opening balance entry posts the same way; only its label differs.

Good to know

  • "A journal entry needs at least two lines."
  • "Debits (85,000.00) must equal credits (80,000.00)." The totals must agree to the centavo.
  • "Choose an account for every journal line." A line with an amount needs an account.
  • "A journal line cannot have both a debit and a credit." Split it into two lines.
  • "Lines posted to Accounts Receivable need a customer name." and "Lines posted to Accounts Payable need a vendor name." Choose the name on that line.
  • A journal entry to Accounts Receivable or Accounts Payable changes the customer's or supplier's balance, but it is not an invoice or a bill: it does not appear in Receive Payments or Pay Bills. Use those modules for sales and purchases.
  • A journal entry never changes item quantities, even when it posts to 1200 Inventory. Use Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand for stock.
  • "The books are closed through 06/28/2026. You do not have permission to record transactions dated on or before the closing date." Only users with the special permission Can change transactions on/before the closing date or already reconciled can post on or before the closing date, or change an entry that was cleared in a bank reconciliation.
  • A voided entry cannot be edited.
  • When the Class at the top is empty but every line that has an account carries its own class, the program saves without asking about the class.
  • Journal entry numbers (JE-) are shared with other entries the program makes itself, such as bank service charges, interest earned and cash over and short, so the numbers on this list may skip.

Financial reports

The reports of this module are listed under Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports in the sidebar, and in the Financial Reports card of the Report Center. They all read the general ledger, so they always agree with each other and with the transactions.

Every report has the common report tools: Refresh, Print / PDF, Export to spreadsheet, Email link, Modify Report and Memorize, and a click on an account or a number opens the detail behind it. These are explained once in Working with reports. This section describes what is special to each report.

Most financial reports have a Class filter. All shows everything; a class shows only the ledger lines of that class; (Unclassified) shows only the lines that have no class.

Trial Balance

The Trial Balance lists every account with a balance on a date, in a Debit or a Credit column, and checks that the two totals agree. Use it at month-end before you prepare the statements.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports > Trial Balance

Filters: As of (the date of the balances) and Class.

The columns: Account (code and name; click it to open its General Ledger), Type, Debit and Credit. The TOTAL row adds each column.

How to read it: balance sheet accounts show their balance at the date. Income and expense accounts show only the activity of the current fiscal year, from the first day of the fiscal year to the As of date, as the note above the table says. The net income of earlier years is included in Retained Earnings. If the two totals differ, the note ends with Out of balance! in red.

Tip

Accounts with a zero balance are left out, so the list stays short.

Balance Sheet

The Balance Sheet shows what the company owns, owes and is worth on a date.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports > Balance Sheet

Filters: As of and Class.

How to read it: the report has three parts. ASSETS has Current Assets (Bank, Accounts Receivable and Other Current Asset accounts), Fixed Assets and Other Assets. LIABILITIES has Current Liabilities (Accounts Payable, Credit Card and Other Current Liability accounts) and Long-term Liabilities. EQUITY lists the equity accounts and the line Net Income (fiscal year to date); Retained Earnings includes the net income of earlier years. Each group has its total, and the last line is TOTAL LIABILITIES AND EQUITY.

Contra accounts show as negative figures: 1525 Accumulated Depreciation - Machinery shows -28,950.00 under Fixed Assets. The note above the table says "Assets = Liabilities + Equity." when the two sides agree.

Note

Filtered by class, the two sides may not agree, because a journal entry or cash voucher can carry a different class on each line. The note then explains this instead of showing an error.

Balance Sheet by Class

The same balance sheet with one column per class, an Unclassified column for entries without a class, and a Total column.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports > Balance Sheet by Class, or the Balance Sheet by Class button on the Class List.

Filters: As of only.

How to read it: every active class has a column, and an inactive class has one when it has entries. Click an amount in a class column to open the General Ledger of that account for that class. The note says "Assets = Liabilities + Equity in total." A single column rarely balances on its own, because a payment received by one branch may settle the invoice of another, or a journal line may carry its own class. The note names the columns that do not balance.

Trial Balance by Date Range

This trial balance shows only the movement of each account between two dates, not the balance carried from before.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports > Trial Balance by Date Range

Filters: Dates (a preset such as This month), From, To and Class.

The columns: Account, Type, and the net movement in Debit or Credit, with a TOTAL row.

How to read it: in the picture, 1010 Cash in Bank - BDO Checking has a debit of 203,828.98: the account went up by that much in September. Use it to review one month's activity or to check a period before you close it.

Trial Balance of Totals

For each account, the total of all debits and the total of all credits posted between two dates, and their difference.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports > Trial Balance of Totals

Filters: Dates, From, To and Class.

The columns: Account, Type, Total debit, Total credit and Net movement (debits less credits). The TOTAL row shows the grand totals; when the books balance, the total debits equal the total credits and the net movement is 0.00.

Example

In September, 1010 Cash in Bank - BDO Checking received 288,828.98 and paid out 85,000.00, a net movement of 203,828.98. The Trial Balance by Date Range shows only the 203,828.98.

Balance Sheet by Date Range

A balance sheet built only from the transactions dated between two dates. Every figure is the change during the period, not the balance at the end of it.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports > Balance Sheet by Date Range

Filters: Dates, From, To and Class.

The columns: Account and Change in the period.

How to read it: assets are shown debit-positive, and liabilities and equity credit-positive, so both sides agree. The Equity section includes Net income of the period. A negative asset figure means the asset went down; in the picture, 1200 Inventory fell by 129,128.33 during September.

Income Statement vs Budget

Puts the actual income and expenses beside an income statement budget and shows the difference.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports > Income Statement vs Budget, the Income statement vs budget button on Set Up Budgets, or Compare with actual on a budget.

Filters:

  • Budget: the budget to compare with. With none chosen, the newest income statement budget is used.
  • Show: Year to date (from the first month of the budget to the month the fiscal year has reached today), The whole fiscal year, or One month at a time.
  • Month: the month of the fiscal year (Month 1 is the first month of the fiscal year), used with One month at a time.
  • Class: shown above the report, but the figures always follow the class of the budget itself.

The columns: Account, Actual, Budget, Difference (actual less budget) and % of budget (how much of the budget was reached). Accounts are grouped under INCOME, COST OF GOODS SOLD, EXPENSES, OTHER INCOME and OTHER EXPENSES, each with a total, and a NET INCOME line closes the report: income and other income, less cost of goods sold, expenses and other expenses. It matches the net income of the Income Statement for the same months. The note names the budget and the months shown.

How to read it: for income, a positive difference is good news; for an expense, a positive difference means you spent more than planned. Click an account to see its entries in the General Ledger.

When no budget exists, the report says "No budget has been set up yet. Financial Set-up & Reports > Set Up Budgets makes one."

Balance Sheet vs Budget

Puts the actual balances of the asset, liability and equity accounts beside a balance sheet budget.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports > Balance Sheet vs Budget, or Compare with actual on a balance sheet budget.

The columns: Account, Actual, Budget, Difference and % of budget, grouped under ASSETS, LIABILITIES and EQUITY with totals.

How to read it: a balance is a figure at a point in time, so the report compares the balances at the end of the last month shown, not a sum of months. With As at the latest month, that is the month the fiscal year has reached today. The equity accounts show only what was posted to them; the profit of the current year is not added to them on this report.

Statement of Cash Flows

The Statement of Cash Flows explains how the cash went up or down in a period, by the indirect method.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports > Statement of Cash Flows

Filters: Dates (starts at Fiscal year to date), From, To and Class.

How to read it:

  • OPERATING ACTIVITIES starts from the Net Income of the period and adds the change in each Accounts Receivable, Other Current Asset, Accounts Payable, Credit Card and Other Current Liability account. The change in the accumulated depreciation accounts is added back as "(depreciation)", because depreciation uses no cash.
  • INVESTING ACTIVITIES shows the change in the Fixed Asset and Other Asset accounts.
  • FINANCING ACTIVITIES shows the change in the Long Term Liability and Equity accounts.
  • NET CASH INCREASE (DECREASE) FOR THE PERIOD, Cash at beginning of period and CASH AT END OF PERIOD close the report.

An increase in an asset uses cash and shows as a negative figure; an increase in a liability or equity provides cash and shows as a positive figure. Only Bank accounts count as cash, as the note "Indirect method. Cash = bank and cash accounts." says. Money still in Undeposited Funds or in the card and e-wallet clearing accounts is treated as an operating asset until it is deposited.

If the ending cash does not agree with the bank balances, the note says "Does not tie to the bank balances" in red with the balance it expected.

General Ledger

The General Ledger lists every entry posted to each account, with a running balance. It is where you land when you click an account on any other report.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports > General Ledger, or click an account name on the Chart of Accounts or on a financial report.

Filters: Dates (starts at This month), From, To, Account (blank for all accounts), Class and Search (a word in the memo, the number or the name).

The columns: Date, Type, Num (click it to open the transaction), Name, Memo, Class, Debit, Credit and Balance (debit +).

How to read it: each account starts with its Beginning balance at the From date and ends with a Total line showing the total debits, total credits and the ending balance. The balance is always shown debit-positive, so an account with a credit balance, such as 2000 Accounts Payable, shows a negative balance. For income and expense accounts, the beginning balance counts only from the start of the fiscal year. Accounts with no entries and no beginning balance are left out.

The Subsidiary Ledger lists the entries of one name (or of every name) on the accounts that keep a subsidiary ledger: a customer, a supplier, an employee or an other name, with a running balance per account.

Filters:

  • Dates, From and To (starts at This fiscal year).

  • Subsidiary ledger (name): one customer, supplier, employee or other name, or — every name —.

Journal

The Journal lists every posted transaction with all its ledger lines, in date order: the book of original entry.

Where to find it: Financial Set-up & Reports > Reports > Journal

Filters: Dates (starts at This month), From, To, Class and Type (one kind of transaction, for example Check or Deposit).

The columns: Date, Type and Num (on the first line of each transaction; click the number to open it), Account, Memo, Class, Debit and Credit. The TOTAL row adds the debits and the credits.

How to read it: each transaction starts on a new line with a rule above it. Use the Type filter to review, for example, all the deposits of the month. With a Class chosen, only the lines of that class are shown. The report shows at most 2,000 transactions; narrow the dates if you need more.

GSMaRT Construction System is a web accounting system. It runs on a server in your office or on the internet, and you use it in a web browser on a computer, a tablet or a phone. Nothing has to be installed on your own device.

This chapter is for everyone who uses the program. It shows how to log in and look after your password, how every page is laid out, and the rules that apply in every module: how forms, lists and printouts work, supervisor approval, pending transactions, voiding and deleting, the closing date, classes and access rights. Read it once before you turn to the chapter for your own work.

About this manual

The manual has one chapter for each module of the program, in the order the modules appear in the menu, and one section for each page. Each page section tells you what the page is for, where to find it, what every box means, how to do each task step by step, what the program records in the books, and the messages you may meet.

Opening the manual from the program

  • Every page has a Help button with a question-mark icon at the right end of its title bar. It opens this manual at the section for that page.
  • The whole manual is also in the user menu: click your name at the top right of the screen, then User's Manual.
  • The log-in page has a User's Manual link, so you can read the manual before you log in.

How the manual is written

  • Words you see on the screen are in bold, exactly as the program shows them: Save & New, Customer:Job, Receive Payments.
  • A menu path such as Customers > Create Invoices means: click Customers in the menu on the left, then click Create Invoices.
  • Boxes marked Note, Tip, Important and Example give extra information, shortcuts, warnings and worked examples.
  • The construction chapter uses the sample contractor, Sample Builders & Development Corp., and its projects. The accounting chapters (the modules under Accounting in the menu) describe each page with examples of a trading business, such as the customer ABC Trading Corp. and the item LAP-001 Laptop; the pages work the same way for a contractor, with projects as classes. Amounts are in pesos, for example ₱12,500.00.
  • What you see depends on the access your role gives you (see "Access: what you can open and do" later in this chapter).
Note

If the manual describes a button or a menu entry that you do not see, your role does not allow that action. Ask your administrator if you need it.

Before you start

You need:

  • A computer, tablet or phone with a current web browser, such as Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.
  • The address of your company's GSMaRT Construction System, for example http://server/accounting-system/. Your administrator gives it to you. Save it as a bookmark.
  • A username and a password. Your administrator creates your account in System Administration > User Accounts. If your company works with a Company Code, you can also create your own account from the log-in page and wait for approval (see Your Company: Company Owner and Employees).

Logging in

The log-in page is the first page you see. It shows the program's name and, under it, the name of your company.

Step by step: log in

  1. Open the address of GSMaRT Construction System in your browser.
  2. Type your Username.
  3. Type your Password. The letters are hidden as you type.
  4. Click Log in, or press Enter.

The Home page opens (see Home Dashboard). If you log in with a temporary password, or your password has expired, the Change password page opens first (see "Your password" below).

Right after you log in you may also see:

  • A pop-up headed Maintenance due that lists the preventive maintenance tasks that are due, if you may open Preventive Maintenance. Click Later to close it, or Open Preventive Maintenance to deal with them. It comes back after each log-in, when a new day starts, and when the list of due tasks changes.
  • A blue message saying that a memorized transaction was entered automatically, for example Memorized transaction "Office rent" was entered automatically for 09/27/2026. Memorized transactions set to be entered automatically are recorded on their due date, the first time anyone opens the program that day.
Note

As the log-in page says, every log-in, every failed attempt and every action is recorded in the audit trail, with the user's name, the time and the computer's network address.

Your password

Your password protects your work and your name in the audit trail. Never tell it to anyone, and never let another person work under your log-in: every entry you save carries your name.

Password rules

Your administrator sets the rules in Company, Security & Closing Date. The Change password page always shows the rules in force. As delivered, a password must have:

  • at least 8 characters,
  • upper-case and lower-case letters,
  • a number,
  • a symbol, such as @ # ! or %,
  • and it must not contain your username.

As delivered, a password expires after 90 days, and a new password must be different from the current one.

Temporary passwords and expired passwords

  • When an administrator creates your account or resets your password, you get a temporary password. At your first log-in the Change password page opens with the message Please replace your temporary password before continuing. Until you change it, every other page brings you back here. Only Log out still works.
  • When your password is older than the number of days set by the administrator, the program shows Your password has expired. Please choose a new one. and opens the same page.

Forgotten password and locked account

The program does not send passwords by e-mail. If you forget your password, or your account is locked after too many wrong passwords (5 as delivered), ask an administrator. In User Accounts they can unlock your account and give you a temporary password, which you then change as described above. A successful log-in sets the count of wrong attempts back to zero.

Logging out and the automatic time-out

To log out, click your name at the top right, then Log out. Always log out when you leave a shared computer.

The program also logs you out by itself when you have not opened or saved a page for a while (30 minutes as delivered; the administrator sets the time). The next time you click, the log-in page opens with You were logged out after a period of inactivity.

Important

Typing in a form does not count as activity. Only opening a page or saving does. If you spend a long time on one form, save it before the time-out runs out, or what you typed is lost when you finally press Save.

The title bar of a page

Under the top bar, every page starts with a title bar. On the left is the page's name, for example Home, New Invoice or Invoice INV-00001. Messages at the top of the page

After you save, void, delete or change something, a coloured message appears above the title bar:

  • green for success, for example Your password has been changed.
  • blue for information, for example a memorized transaction that was entered automatically,
  • yellow for a warning, for example You were logged out after a period of inactivity.
  • red for a problem that stopped the action.

Click the small cross at the right of a message to close it. The message goes away by itself when you open another page.

When a form cannot be saved, a red box with a warning sign appears above the form and says why, for example Choose an item for every line that has a description or amount. Everything you typed stays on the form. Correct what the message asks and save again.

Working with forms

Every transaction in the program (an invoice, a bill, a check, a journal entry, an inventory adjustment) is entered on a form. The forms work the same way everywhere. The picture shows a new invoice as an example.

Opening a form

  • From the menu: most document pages, such as Customers > Create Invoices, open on the list of that kind of transaction; click New in the title bar for a blank form. Some pages, such as Customers > Receive Payments, open on the form itself.
  • From the Home page: the Quick actions buttons open the page, and for the common documents (estimates, sales orders, invoices, sales receipts, statement charges, credit memos, bills, purchase orders, checks and journal entries) they open a blank form directly.
  • To change a saved transaction, open it and click Edit.

Typing in the boxes

  • Dates are shown as month/day/year, for example 09/27/2026. Type the date or click the calendar button in the box to pick it. A new form starts with today's date.
  • Amounts are typed without the peso sign. You may type commas (12,500.00) or leave them out (12500). The program keeps two decimals.
  • Number boxes that show a greyed-out number with (auto), such as INV-00014 (auto), fill themselves in when you save. Leave them empty unless you need a number of your own.
  • Boxes whose label says what happens when they are left blank, such as Due date (blank = from terms), can be left empty.

Drop-downs you can type in

Any drop-down with more than four choices (customers, suppliers, items, accounts, classes, terms and so on) is a box you can type in. It shows Type to search until something is chosen.

  1. Click the box, or move into it with the Tab key. The list of choices opens under it, with the current choice highlighted.
  2. Type any part of what you are looking for. The list narrows with every letter. Several words may be typed in any order: every word must appear somewhere in the choice. Choices that begin with what you typed come first, and the matching letters are marked.
  3. Click the choice you want, or move to it with the Up and Down arrow keys and press Enter.

Good to know:

  • After you have typed, leaving the box with the Tab key takes the highlighted choice.
  • Esc closes the list and keeps what was chosen before.
  • To empty a box, delete the text and leave the box, or open the list and click the first entry (for example Clear this box), which appears when something is chosen.
  • If nothing matches, the list says Nothing matches followed by what you typed.
  • The part in brackets at the end of a choice, such as an account code or the kind of name, is shown in lighter letters.
  • Short lists, such as a status or a yes-or-no choice, stay ordinary drop-downs.
Example

On the invoice, click Customer:Job and type abc. The list shrinks to ABC Trading Corp. and its jobs, such as ABC Trading Corp.:Office Renovation Project. Press Enter to take the first one.

The lines of a transaction

The middle of most forms is a grid of lines, one for each item, service or account.

  • Choose the Item (or the account). The program fills in the description, the unit, the price (the cost on purchase forms) and the tax code from the item, and a quantity of 1.
  • Amount is worked out as Qty times Rate, less the Discount. The discount is either a percentage (%) or an amount off the line (Amt): choose which in the small drop-down beside the discount box.
  • If you type the Amount yourself, the Rate is worked out from it.
  • The tax of each line comes from its tax code. Prices on sales and purchase forms are VAT-exclusive: the VAT is added on top.
  • Click Add line for another line, or Add 5 lines or Add 10 lines to add that many empty lines in one click. The new lines are lit up for a moment.
  • To put a line in between two others, click the small square plus button at the end of the line that should come after it: an empty line is inserted just above that line, and the lines keep that order when the form is saved.
  • Click the red cross at the end of a line to remove it.
  • On forms with a site at the top (Default site for items on bills, item receipts and purchase orders, Inventory site on sales forms), the site chosen there is copied onto every line, including the lines added later, so you do not have to choose it on each line. A line can still be given another site. Changing the site at the top afterwards moves every line that still had the old one, and leaves alone the lines you changed yourself.
  • There is no set limit to the number of lines: a bill or an invoice of several hundred lines is saved whole, and editing it later keeps every line. Long forms are sent to the server in one package for this reason.

The Subtotal, Tax and Total under the grid add up as you type. A negative total is shown in brackets, for example (1,250.00).

Saving

The buttons at the foot of the form stay in view while you scroll:

  • Save records the transaction and shows it as saved.
  • Save & New records it and opens a blank form, so you can enter the next one straight away. It appears only if you may create transactions on the page.
  • Cancel leaves without saving.

If something is missing or wrong, nothing is saved and a red box above the form tells you what to correct.

If a very long form shows the message "This form has more fields than the server accepts at once (...), so nothing was saved", the page was opened before the program was updated: press Ctrl+F5 to load it again, enter the lines and save. Nothing was saved by the attempt.

Stock below zero: the Yes / No question

When Allow selling or moving stock below zero (with warning) is switched on (see "Stock below zero" in Inventory), saving a sales invoice, a sales receipt, a batch of invoices, a POS sale, an inventory adjustment or an inventory transfer that would leave an item below zero at a site is not done straight away. A window headed Stock will go below zero says "Saving the transaction will lead the inventory to a negative balance." It lists each item with its site, the quantity On hand and the quantity After saving, and asks Do you want to continue?

  • Click Yes to save the transaction. The same button you pressed is used again, so Save & New still opens a blank form afterwards.
  • Click No to go back to the form. Nothing has been saved; change the quantities, or cancel the transaction.

A yellow box above the form repeats the items while you decide. When the setting is switched off, there is no question: the transaction is refused with "Not enough stock: ...".

Memorize Transaction and Load Memorized Transaction

A transaction you enter again and again (a monthly retainer invoice, the rent check, an accrual journal entry, the regular order from a supplier) can be kept as a pro-forma and loaded into a new form whenever it is needed. The forms that have it: Create Estimates, Create Sales Orders, Create Invoices, Create Batch Invoices, Enter Sales Receipts, Enter Statement Charges, Create Credit Memos, Enter Bills, Supplier Debit Memos, Create Purchase Orders, Receive Items, Receive Items and Enter Bills, Write Checks, Cash Voucher, Make Journal Entries and Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand.

To memorize what is on the form:

  1. Fill in the form as if you were going to save it.
  2. Click Memorize Transaction at the right of the save buttons.
  3. In the box, type a name in Name it, a Group if you use groups, and choose the Reminder: Don't remind me keeps it on the list until you load it; Remind me when it is due (or Enter it automatically) asks for Every, Next due and Remind (days before).
  4. Click Memorize Transaction in the box. The form is checked as for saving; the message "Memorized as "...". It is on the Memorized Transactions list ... This invoice itself was not saved." appears and the form stays filled, so you can still save it.

To fill a new form from it:

  1. Open the form for a new transaction and click Load Memorized Transaction in the title bar (it shows how many there are for this form).
  2. Type part of the name to find it, and click Load. If you had already typed something on the form, you are asked "Replace what is on the form with the memorized transaction?".
  3. The form opens with the names, items, accounts, amounts, site, class and memo that were memorized, under the note "Loaded from the memorized transaction ...". The date and the document number are today's and the next ones. Change what differs and save.

Memorizing records nothing in the books, and no supervisor approval is asked for it; the approval is asked when the transaction is saved. The memorized transactions are on Memorized Transactions, where they can also be entered, rescheduled or taken off.

The Class box and the "No class selected" question

Every transaction form has a Class box. Classes are your own way of dividing the books: branches, departments, projects or product lines. They are kept in the Class List. The class you choose is copied to every ledger line the transaction posts, so each financial report can be filtered by class, and the "by Class" statements show one column per class. Journal entries and cash vouchers can also carry a different class on each line.

When classes have been set up and you save a transaction without one, the program asks first. A window headed No class selected says: "This transaction has no class. It will be saved as Unclassified and will not appear under any class on the class reports."

  • Click Choose a class to go back; the Class box is marked so you find it quickly.
  • Click Continue saving to save it without a class.

On reports, transactions without a class are grouped as (Unclassified). When no class has been set up yet, the Class box says No classes yet (Class List) and the question is not asked.

Supervisor approval

Some actions go beyond what your role allows on its own, so a second person must approve them. The program then shows Supervisor approval is required: followed by the reason, for example "15.00% discount on LAP-001". It happens when:

  • a discount on a line is larger than your Max discount % without approval,
  • a price is changed from the standard price by more than your Max price change % without approval,
  • a check, bill payment, cash voucher, refund, inventory adjustment, count adjustment or processing order is larger than your Max check / bill payment / adjustment amount without approval,
  • a check, bill payment or refund above that limit is voided or deleted,
  • at the counter: discounts and price changes beyond the cashier's limits, returns and refunds, voids of POS sales, X readings and closing the register (Z reading).

The Supervisor approval section has two tabs. Supervisor is here is for a supervisor standing beside you, who types their own username and password. Approval code is for a supervisor who is somewhere else: they generate a one-time code on Approval Codes and read it out to you, and you type it instead of their password.

Step by step: get an approval

  1. Save as usual. The form comes back with the reason and the Supervisor approval section open, marked required.
  2. Either ask a supervisor to type their username in Supervisor username and their password in Password (the password is hidden as it is typed), or open the Approval code tab and type the eight-letter code the supervisor gave you, for example AC7K-9MXT. The dash is put in for you, and small letters are read as capitals.
  3. Click Save again.

The approver's name is recorded on the transaction (Approved by) and in the audit trail, whichever way the approval was given: a code stands for the supervisor who generated it. A code works once: the moment the transaction is saved with it, it is spent, and if the save fails for another reason the code is given back so you can try again with it.

Pending transactions

Invoices and sales receipts have a tick box Pending (non-posting until marked final). A pending sale is saved as a document but does not post to the books or take items out of stock. Use it, for example, while a customer's purchase order is being approved.

  • A pending transaction shows the yellow note "This transaction is pending. It does not affect the books or inventory until it is marked final." Lists show it with the status Pending.
  • To make it final, open it and click Mark as final. The form opens with Pending unticked and the note Pending is unticked — saving posts this sale. Click Save.
  • The credit limit of the customer is checked when the invoice becomes final, not while it is pending.
  • The Home page counts the pending sales, and the Pending Sales report lists them.

Assembly builds and processing orders can also be saved as pending when components are short; see Build Assemblies and Disassemble / Process Items.

Estimates, sales orders and purchase orders never post: they record a commitment only, and their page says "Non-posting document: it records a commitment and does not affect the ledger." Suspended POS sales do not post either until they are recalled and completed.

Questions before an action

Some buttons ask before they act, in a small window of the browser, for example Close this document? Remaining quantities will no longer be expected. Click OK to go on or Cancel to stop.

Other buttons carry the document to its next step, such as Create Invoice on a sales order, Receive Payment on an invoice, Pay Bill on a bill, or Close and Reopen on estimates and orders. They are explained in the chapter for each page.

The message that Email prepares holds a link to the printout. Only someone who can log in to GSMaRT Construction System can open that link. To send a document to a customer, print it to PDF (see below) and attach the file to the message.

Printing and saving as PDF

Print on a saved transaction opens its printout on a plain page, without the menus, with two buttons at the top: Print and Back.

  1. Click Print on the saved transaction.
  2. Click Print at the top of the printout. The browser's print window opens.
  3. Choose your printer, or choose Save as PDF (the name differs slightly between browsers) to make a PDF file.
  4. Click Back to return to the transaction.

The buttons at the top do not appear on paper. Opening the printout of a check, bill payment or refund marks it as printed, and every printout is recorded in the audit trail. Reports print the same way; see Working with reports.

Voiding and deleting

A saved transaction that is wrong can be corrected with Edit. When it should not exist at all, you void it or delete it.

  • Void keeps the document, with its number, but removes its effect on the ledger and on inventory. It stays in the lists, struck through, with the status Void, and its page shows VOID with the reason and the time. A voided transaction cannot be edited. Use it when the document was issued, for example a spoiled check.
  • Delete removes the document completely. Its full content is kept in the audit trail. Use it for an entry made by mistake that was never issued.

Estimates, sales orders and purchase orders do not post, so they cannot be voided; delete them or close them. POS sales cannot be deleted, only voided.

Step by step: void or delete a transaction

  1. Open the transaction.
  2. Click Void (or Delete).
  3. Type the reason in Reason (required, recorded in the audit trail).
  4. If the action needs approval, let a supervisor type their username and password under Supervisor approval, or type the one-time approval code they gave you.
  5. Click Void transaction (or Delete transaction).

The program says, for example, Invoice INV-00005 was voided. or Invoice INV-00005 was deleted. A full copy is kept in the audit trail.

Message What to do

The Void and Delete buttons appear only if your role has the Delete/Void right on the page. The Voided and Deleted Transactions Summary report lists every void and deletion, so they can be reviewed.

The closing date

When a period's books are final, the administrator sets a closing date in Company, Security & Closing Date. The Home page then says, for example, Books closed through 07/09/2026.

From then on, transactions dated on or before the closing date can no longer be entered, edited, voided or deleted, except by users whose role has the special permission Can change transactions on/before the closing date or already reconciled. The same protection covers transactions already cleared in a bank reconciliation.

Message What it means
The books are closed through 07/09/2026. You do not have permission to record transactions dated on or before the closing date. Use a date after the closing date, or ask an authorised user.
This transaction is dated on or before the closing date (07/09/2026) and cannot be changed. The transaction belongs to a closed period.
This transaction has been cleared in a bank reconciliation. You do not have permission to change it. It was ticked as cleared in Reconcile.
Tip

A mistake found in a closed period is usually corrected with an entry dated in the open period, for example a journal entry, so that the figures already reported for the closed period stay as they were.

Access: what you can open and do

Every user has one or more roles, and the roles decide what the user may do. The administrator manages them in User Role Configuration and Page Access Configuration.

  • Opening a page (View). A page appears in your menu, and can be opened, only if one of your roles allows it. A user override set by the administrator can allow or deny a single page for you alone; an override always wins over the roles.
  • Actions on a page. For each page a role allows Create, Edit, Delete/Void, Print/Export and Approve separately. A button whose action you may not take does not appear: without Print/Export you see no Print or Export buttons, without Delete/Void no Void or Delete buttons.
  • Special permissions. Some rights are set per role, apart from the pages:
Special permission What it controls
View costs and margins Cost and margin columns on forms and reports.
View bank balances outside Banking Bank balances on the Home page, the Chart of Accounts and Transfer Funds.
Max discount % without approval Larger discounts need supervisor approval.
Max price change % without approval Larger price changes need supervisor approval.
Max check / bill payment / adjustment amount without approval Larger amounts need supervisor approval. Blank means no limit.
Can exceed customer credit limits Saving an invoice over the credit limit, with a reason.
Can change the Chart of Accounts and default accounts Changes to accounts and module default accounts.
Can change transactions on/before the closing date or already reconciled Work in closed periods and on reconciled lines.
Can design report layouts (fonts, headings and footers, columns and rows) and save or share them The Modify Report button on reports.
  • Sites and POS terminals. A user or a role can be limited to some inventory sites and POS terminals. Such users can only record transactions for their sites, and lists, site choices and site-based reports show only their sites.
  • Administrators can open everything. In a company with a Company Owner, the owner can always open every page of the company, whatever the roles say.

When you try to open a page you may not use, the Access denied page says "You do not have permission to open" followed by the page's name. The attempt is recorded in the audit trail. Click Go to Home or Go back, and ask your administrator if you need the access. Administrators can check exactly what a user may do, and why, in Effective Access (View as User).

Using GSMaRT Construction System on a phone or tablet

Every page is laid out for small screens as well. On a phone the top bar holds the menu button (three lines) at the left, the program's name, the Find button (funnel icon), the bell and your name. The search box is left out for lack of room; use Find, whose page has a Search names and items button. The title bar of the page stacks its buttons full width, and cards such as the Home figures sit one under the other.

Tap the menu button to open the menu. It slides in as a panel headed Menu, with the same modules as on a computer. Tap a page to open it, or tap the cross at the top right to close the panel.

Forms put their boxes one under the other, and the Save, Save & New and Cancel buttons stay at the foot of the screen.

Good to know on small screens:

  • A table wider than the screen scrolls sideways inside its own box: swipe the table, not the page. A soft shadow on the right edge shows that more columns are out of sight. Report lines stay on one line so they can be followed across; only long memos and descriptions wrap.
  • Boxes and buttons are large enough to tap, and text boxes are sized so the phone does not zoom in when you tap one.
  • The list of a type-to-search box is drawn in the part of the screen the keyboard leaves free, with the phone held upright or on its side.
  • On the Find page, each transaction found is shown as a small block, and a drop-down replaces the sortable column headings.
  • The POS Register has its own phone layout; see Point of Sale.

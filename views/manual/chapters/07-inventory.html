The Inventory module holds the Item List, the list of everything you buy and sell, and keeps the quantity and value of the goods you stock at each of your sites. From it you move stock between sites, correct quantities and values, count the stock, build assemblies, take items apart or process them into several products, and keep your selling prices and price levels up to date. The inventory reports at the end of the chapter show what you have, where it is, what it is worth and what needs to be ordered.

Buying and selling are done in other modules: goods come in through purchase orders, Receive Items and bills (chapter 6, Vendors and Payables) and go out through invoices, sales receipts (chapter 5, Customers and Receivables) and the counter (Point of Sale). Each of those documents uses the items described here and changes the stock at the site named on it.

How inventory is kept

A few rules explain almost everything in this chapter.

  • Only two item types are stocked. Inventory parts and inventory assemblies have a quantity on hand and a value. They are called tracked items. Services, non-inventory parts and the other types are sold and bought, but no stock is kept for them.
  • Stock is kept per site. A site is a place where goods are kept: a warehouse, a store, a stockroom, a delivery van. Every receipt, sale, transfer, adjustment and build names a site, and the quantity on hand of each item is known for each site. You set up the sites in the Inventory Site List.
  • Costing is weighted average. Each tracked item has one average cost for all sites together. The average is always the remaining value of the item divided by its remaining quantity, both counted from the same movements (every receipt, sale, transfer, adjustment, build and processing order the item was in). A purchase at a different cost changes the average; a sale, transfer, adjustment down or build takes goods out at the current average.
  • The books follow the stock. Every document that changes the value of stock also posts to the general ledger: the item's inventory asset account (for example 1200 Inventory), cost of goods sold (5000 Cost of Goods Sold) and, for adjustments, 5100 Inventory Adjustments and Shrinkage. The Inventory Valuation Summary compares the stock value with the ledger balance, so you can see that they agree.
  • The last unit takes the last peso. When a sale or issue takes the last unit of an item, all the value that is left goes out with it, so no value stays behind on an item with no stock.
Example

You have 10 USB-032 USB Flash Drive 32GB at ₱180.00 each, a value of ₱1,800.00. You receive 10 more at ₱170.00 (₱1,700.00). The average cost becomes ₱3,500.00 ÷ 20 = ₱175.00. When you sell 3, cost of goods sold is 3 × ₱175.00 = ₱525.00, and the 17 left are worth ₱2,975.00, still ₱175.00 each.

Stock below zero

A sale can take more than the site has, for example when goods were delivered before the supplier's delivery receipt was entered. The quantity at that site then goes below zero. The issue beyond the stock is costed at the last average cost.

The receipt that brings the stock back (a bill, an item receipt, the outputs of a processing order, a build, an adjustment up) settles that shortfall at its own cost. The difference between what the short units were costed at and what they really cost goes to cost of goods sold, and what is left on hand carries the cost of the receipt. The settlement shows in the item's history as a line with no quantity under the receipt, marked Below-zero stock settled at this cost, and in the receipt's journal as "Stock sold below zero, settled at the cost received". A transfer between sites does not settle anything: it is the same stock moving.

Example

The counter sold 99.9 kg of PK-CHOP Pork Chop but only 90 kg were processed: 9.9 kg went out below zero at the ₱225.00 average (₱2,227.50). The day's processing order then puts in 10 kg at ₱300.00 (₱3,000.00). The 9.9 kg really cost ₱300.00, so ₱742.50 more goes to cost of goods sold, and the 0.1 kg left is worth ₱30.00: the average is ₱300.00. Before version 31 the whole difference stayed on the 0.1 kg and the average became ₱7,725.00.

The setting Allow selling or moving stock below zero (with warning), in System Administration > Company, Security & Closing Date under Inventory and POS (see Company, Security & Closing Date), decides what happens when a document would take an item's quantity at a site below zero. It applies to every document that takes stock out: invoices, sales receipts, POS sales, supplier debit memos that return items, transfers, adjustments and any other stock-out document, when it is saved, edited or marked final.

  • Switched on (the standard): on sales invoices, sales receipts, batch invoices, POS sales, inventory adjustments and transfers the program asks first: "Saving the transaction will lead the inventory to a negative balance. Do you want to continue?", with the items, their quantity on hand and what would be left. Yes saves the document, No goes back to it with nothing saved (see Stock below zero: the Yes / No question). Once saved, and on the other documents straight away, a yellow message names each item now below zero, for example "Stock is now below zero: LAP-001 at Main Warehouse (-2.00)."
  • Switched off: the document is not saved, and the message says what is missing, for example "Not enough stock: LAP-001 at Main Warehouse has 3.00 on hand, and this would leave -2.00. Selling or moving stock below zero is switched off (System Administration > Company, Security & Closing Date)." Sell or move less, or record the missing receipt first. A processing order whose outputs were already used cannot be voided or deleted either.

Only a save that lowers the quantity is checked: changing the memo or the price of an older document while the item happens to be below zero is still allowed. Utilities: Verify & Rebuild Data re-posts history without this check.

Build Assemblies and Disassemble / Process Items never issue parts that are short, whatever the setting: they offer to save the document as pending instead. Watch the Negative Inventory Listing report and the "negative stock record(s)" figure on the Home dashboard, and record the missing receipt, transfer or adjustment. Utilities: Verify & Rebuild Data also warns about negative quantities (see Utilities: Verify & Rebuild Data).

Who sees costs

Costs are sensitive. A user sees the Cost, Avg cost, value and margin figures only when one of their roles has the special permission View costs and margins (see User Role Configuration). Without it, those columns and fields are hidden on the pages and reports of this chapter, and the Total value and Quantity and total value adjustments and the markup on cost are not offered.

A user can also be limited to some sites (see User Accounts). Such a user sees and records stock only for the sites they are allowed.

How items are shown in the lists

The item drop-downs of the transaction forms show both the item name/code (for example LAP-001) and the display name (Laptop 14-inch i5 16GB). System Administration > Company, Security & Closing Date > Searching for items decides which comes first, for all transactions at once or for each transaction on its own: Item name/code + Display name (LAP-001 - Laptop 14-inch i5 16GB) or Display name + Item name/code (Laptop 14-inch i5 16GB - LAP-001). The two item columns of Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand, Physical Inventory Count, Change Item Prices and Set Item Prices and Levels follow the same order. Whatever the order, typing any part of either one finds the item.

How the special types behave on a sales form

  • Subtotal: shows on screen the total of the lines above it, up to the previous subtotal. Its own amount is zero and it posts nothing. It prints on the document without a quantity.
  • Group: a group is sold on one line: the group, how many, and its Group price (see Item groups: group price and price variance). The document can then be shown and edited by item group or by item details. On a purchase form the members come in at their cost.
  • Discount: a percentage discount (for example DISC-10, 10%) takes that percentage of the amount of the line directly above it; a fixed discount (DISC-AMT, ₱100.00) takes off the amount in its Fixed discount amount (you can type another amount on the line). The discount line posts as a negative amount to the item's Discount account (4060 Sales Discounts in the demo), so it is recorded as a debit there.
  • Payment: records money received on the document itself. The line reduces the document total and posts as a debit to 1050 Undeposited Funds, to be deposited later in Make Deposits. A payment line carries no tax.

Step by step: create an inventory part

  1. Open Inventory > Item List and click New item > Inventory part.
  2. Type the Item name / code (for example BAG-002) and the Display name.
  3. Type or scan the Barcode if the product has one.
  4. Choose the Unit of measure (U/M set) or type the Unit, and the Category.
  5. Under Purchase information, type the Cost and pick the Preferred vendor. Leave the Cost of goods sold account as it is unless your accountant asks otherwise.
  6. Under Sales information, type the Sales price and choose the Tax code (VAT12 for VAT-able goods).
  7. Under Inventory information, type the Reorder point (min) and Maximum stock.
  8. If the goods are already on your shelves, type the opening Quantity on hand and its Total value, the As of date and the Site.
  9. Fill the Price levels boxes only for levels that need a fixed price.
  10. Click Save item. The message "Item saved." appears.

Step by step: create an assembly

  1. Click New item > Inventory assembly.
  2. Fill the first section, the sales price, tax code, reorder point, maximum stock and Build point.
  3. Under Bill of materials, pick the first component in Item and type its Qty per assembly.
  4. Click Add component for each other component, including labor as a service item.
  5. Check Total component cost, then click Save item.
  6. Build the stock with Build Assemblies. An assembly has no opening quantity on its form. For assemblies you already had before you started, use Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand with Quantity and total value, so they come in with a value.

Step by step: create a group, discount, subtotal or payment item

  1. Click New item and pick Group, Discount, Subtotal or Payment.
  2. Type the Item name / code and Display name.
  3. For a group, add the member items and their quantities under Items in this group, then type the Group price and choose the Price variance GL account (or leave the module default).
  4. For a discount, type either the Discount % (0 = fixed amount) or the Fixed discount amount, and check the Discount account.
  5. Type a Sales description (it is printed on the documents).
  6. Click Save item.

Step by step: change or retire an item

  1. Click the item's Code on the list (tick Include inactive to find an inactive one).
  2. Change the fields and click Save item. Changes apply to new documents; saved documents keep their own figures.
  3. To retire the item, untick Active and save. Items cannot be deleted, because documents still refer to them.

Processing Formulas (Multi-Output BOM)

A processing formula is a reusable recipe for turning one or more inputs into several outputs: taking an item apart, opening a box to sell the contents one by one, or processing raw material into a main product and by-products. It says what goes in, what comes out and how the cost is shared, for one batch. A processing order multiplies the formula by the number of batches you run.

Where to find it: Inventory > Processing Formulas (Multi-Output BOM). The Formulas and Processing formulas buttons on the processing pages open it too.

The list

The list shows the Code, Name, process Type, Allocation method, Inputs (per batch), Outputs (per batch) (by-products and scrap are marked), the number of Orders that used it and its Status. Choose Active formulas, Inactive formulas or All formulas in the box above the table.

  • Run: starts a new processing order with the formula loaded.
  • New formula: opens a blank formula.
  • New from assembly BOM: pick an assembly in the box beside it and click the button. The new formula is filled from its bill of materials: code UNB- plus the assembly code, type Disassembly / Unbuild, method Relative standard cost, the assembly as input and its stocked components as outputs.
  • Processing orders: opens the list of processing orders.
Note

You do not need a formula to take an assembly apart: a processing order can load any assembly's bill of materials directly.

Fields

Field What to enter
Code Up to 30 characters, saved in capitals, unique.
Name What the formula does, for example Break bulk: USB 32GB box of 50.
Process type Disassembly / Unbuild, De-kitting / Break-bulk or Co-product / By-product.
Usual processing site The site orders start on. Any leaves it to the person running it.
Cost allocation method How the cost is shared among the outputs (see the table below). The help text under the box explains the method chosen.
Description / instructions Notes for the people doing the work.
Active Untick it to stop the formula being chosen for new orders.

Inputs per batch: the Item consumed (an inventory part, assembly, non-inventory part, service or other charge), a Description and the Qty per batch. Est. cost is filled when you check the allocation.

Additional costs per batch (optional): labor, machine time or overhead added to the cost. Account credited starts on the module default account for absorbed processing costs (5200 Direct Labor Absorbed in the demo); type a Description and the Amount per batch.

Outputs per batch: the Item produced (inventory parts and assemblies only), a Description, the Role (Co-product (main), By-product or Scrap / waste), the Qty per batch and the Basis. Share, Est. cost and Unit cost are filled when you check the allocation.

The cost allocation methods

By-products and scrap are valued first, at their Basis (a value per unit; scrap is usually 0). The rest of the cost goes to the co-products by the method:

Method How the co-products share the cost Basis on a co-product
Relative sales value In proportion to quantity × selling price Price per unit; blank uses the sales price
Relative standard cost In proportion to quantity × standard cost Cost per unit; blank uses the item cost
Physical units Every unit produced carries the same cost Not used
Weight / measure In proportion to quantity × weight or volume per unit Weight per unit (required)
Fixed percentages Each gets its percentage; they must add up to 100 The percentage
Current average cost (difference to variance) Each is valued at its average cost; the difference goes to the variance account Value per unit; blank uses the average cost
Manual amounts (difference to variance) Each gets the amount you give; the difference goes to the variance account Total cost of the line per batch

With the first five methods the outputs always carry the whole cost. The last two value the outputs and post any difference from the cost to the processing variance account.

Step by step: create a formula

  1. Click New formula.
  2. Type the Code and Name, choose the Process type, the Usual processing site and the Cost allocation method.
  3. Under Inputs per batch, choose each item and its Qty. Click Add input for more.
  4. Under Additional costs per batch (optional), enter the labor or overhead, or leave the line empty.
  5. Under Outputs per batch, choose each item, its Role and Qty, and a Basis where the method needs one. Click Add output for more.
  6. Click Check allocation. The shares and costs are worked out with today's costs, and a blue box gives the total cost per batch, the by-products and scrap, the amount allocated and, for the variance methods, the variance.
  7. Click Save formula (or Save & New). The message "Formula ... saved." appears.

Buttons on a saved formula

  • Run this formula: starts a processing order with it.
  • Deactivate or Activate: a deactivated formula "stays on past orders but cannot be chosen for new ones".
  • Delete: only for a formula never used on an order; you must type a reason, and a full copy is kept in the audit trail.
  • Yield analysis: for a used formula, opens the Processing Yield Analysis report on it.
  • Formula list: back to the list.

A formula already used shows "Used on n processing order(s). Changes apply to new orders only; posted orders keep their own lines."

Good to know

  • "Add at least one input item (what is being processed or taken apart)." and "Add at least one output item (what the process produces)."
  • "... cannot be an output. Outputs must be inventory or assembly items so their quantity and value can be tracked."
  • "... is both an input and an output. Use an inventory adjustment for rework of the same item." and "The same output item is listed twice. Combine the lines."
  • "Mark at least one output as a co-product (main product)." The five sharing methods need one.
  • "The co-product percentages must add up to 100% ..." and "Enter the weight or measure per unit (basis) for ...".
  • "By-product and scrap values (...) are more than the total cost to allocate (...). Lower their values per unit."
  • "Formula ... is used on n processing order(s). Deactivate it instead."

How processing orders move the average cost

A processing order is often where an item's average cost changes most, because it does two things at once: it takes the inputs out at their average cost, and it puts the outputs in at their share of that cost. Five things decide what it does to the averages.

  1. The inputs go out at the average of all sites. Whatever the input's average is when the order is saved passes into the outputs. If the input's average is wrong, every output inherits it: a carcass at an overstated ₱462.69 instead of ₱225.00 makes every cut about twice as dear.
  2. Each output takes its share of the cost. The share follows the Allocation method of the order or its formula: with Physical units or Weight / measure every kilo gets the same unit cost, trimmings as much as the chops; with Relative sales value the dearer cuts take more; with Fixed percentages the shares never change. When the input cost, the shares or the yields change from one order to the next, the unit cost of an output changes with them, and so does its average. A formula with relative sales value or fixed percentages keeps the unit costs steadier. With Current average cost (difference to variance) the outputs come in at their own average, which then does not move at all; the difference goes to the processing variance account.
  3. Outputs received while they are below zero. When the counter sells cuts before the processing order is entered, the cuts go below zero at their last average. The order then settles that shortfall at its own unit cost and charges the difference to cost of goods sold (see Stock below zero). Before version 31 the difference stayed on the few units left and could multiply the average.
  4. Inputs used before the bill is entered. The same happens to the input: processed before the supplier's bill is in, it goes below zero, and the bill settles the shortfall. Entering the bill first, or on the same date (the stock received is counted first on each date), keeps the cuts at the bill's cost.
  5. Back-dated and edited orders. A saved order is costed at the averages of the moment it is saved, not of its date, and saving an old order again issues its inputs at today's average. Utilities: Verify & Rebuild Data works every cost out again in date order.

The Average Cost Changes report lists each of these as it happened, with the average before and after; tick Processing, disassembly and builds only to see the processing orders alone.

Note

Version 31 fixes a fault that made averages jump after processing orders. A processing order that uses all of a bill leaves the site at zero, and Rebuild Data removed such zero rows from the quantities table. When the order or the bill was saved again later, the quantity at that site was not taken down, so the table held less than the movements (the Inventory Center showed "The quantity on hand (-4.81) differs from the total of its movements (91.86) by -96.67"), and every receipt after that divided the value by too small a quantity: bills at ₱225.00 showed averages of ₱625.64 and ₱462.69. The average is now always worked out on the quantity of the movements, and the upgrade script puts the quantities table right. Run Rebuild Data once after the upgrade so the orders and sales costed at the overstated averages are costed again.

Inventory Movement Summary Report

How the stock of each item moved over a period: what it had at the start, what came in, what went out and what it had at the end. Use it to explain the change in stock from one date to another, site by site, and to trace any figure to the transactions behind it.

Where to find it: Inventory > Reports > Inventory Movement Summary Report. It is given to the roles that may open No. of Days Inventory Has No Movement.

Filters: Dates with a From date and time and a To date and time (this month as delivered), Item, Site, Class, Category, Cashier and Shift. The site, class and item filters narrow the beginning balance and the movements alike. With Class, the movements of the transactions (or the lines) of that class count; (Unclassified) keeps those without a class. The times, the cashier and the shift are explained in Dates with times, cashier and shift.

Column Meaning
Item code, Item description The inventory part or assembly. Click the code to open it in the Inventory Center.
Beg. balance The ending inventory of the day before the From date: every movement up to the end of that day.
In What came into stock in the period: Write Checks and Cash Vouchers with inventory (whether the quantity is positive or negative), Receive Items, Receive Items and Enter Bills, Transfer Inventory to the site, Adjust Quantity (the quantity added), Build Assemblies (the assembled items) and Disassemble / Process Items (the outputs).
Out What went out of stock in the period: Create Invoices, Create Batch Invoices, Enter Sales Receipts, POS sales (and statement charges), less credit memos and POS returns that restock, Transfer Inventory from the site, Adjust Quantity (the quantity taken off), Build Assemblies (the component items), Disassemble / Process Items (the inputs consumed) and Supplier Debit Memos.
Ending balance The ending inventory of the To date: Beg. balance + In - Out.

Only transactions dated within the range count in In and Out, and voided transactions are left out. The TOTAL line adds up the four columns. With every site, a transfer counts as In at the site it went to and as Out at the site it left, so it does not change the total; with one site chosen, only that site's side counts. Only the items with a figure are listed, inactive items that moved included.

Every figure, the TOTAL line included, can be drilled down: click it to open Inventory Movement Transactions with the source transactions of that figure.

Dates with times, cashier and shift

A business open 24 hours with three shifts (for example 12:01 AM - 8:00 AM, 8:01 AM - 2:00 PM and 2:01 PM - 12:00 AM) can see the beginning inventory, the movements and the ending inventory of each shift. Each date has a time box beside it:

Box What it does
From date and time The time the period starts, from the beginning of that minute. Empty means the start of the day.
To date and time The time the period ends, to the end of that minute. Empty means the end of the day. For 2:01 PM - 12:00 AM, type the To date as the next day at 12:00 AM, or leave the To time empty.
Cashier A cashier: the site becomes the outlet the cashier worked at in the period (the site of the terminal of the cashier's shifts, or the cashier's default POS site), so the report shows the stock the cashier is answerable for.
Shift One POS shift (the list shows the cashier, the terminal, its site, and the time it was opened and closed): the dates and times are set to the moment the shift was opened and the moment it was closed (now, for a shift still open), with its cashier and site. Change the dates afterwards if needed; they are kept.

How a transaction is placed in time: on its own date, at the time of day it was recorded. A POS sale is recorded as it is rung up, so it falls in the shift that made it. A delivery entered later and dated an earlier day counts on that day, at the time of day it was entered. The ending inventory of one shift is always the beginning inventory of the next, and the IN and OUT of the three shifts add up to those of the whole day. The heading shows the dates with their times (10/09/2026 08:01 AM through 10/09/2026 02:00 PM), and a drill-down keeps the times and shows the Time recorded of each transaction.

Example

Hotdogs started September with 15 packs. 100 came in (purchases, a transfer and an adjustment) and 87 went out (sales, a transfer out and a build). The report shows Beg. balance 15, In 100, Out 87 and Ending balance 28. Click 100 to see the purchases, the transfer and the adjustment that make it up.

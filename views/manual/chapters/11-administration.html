The System Administration module is where you decide who may use GSMaRT Construction System and what each person may do. It also holds the company's details and security rules, the closing date, the data checks and repairs, the spreadsheet import, backup and restore, the one-time multi-company setup, and the audit reports that show who did what.

Most of these pages are meant for the system administrator. Two standard roles use a part of them: the Auditor reads the audit reports, and the Accountant can run the data checks on Utilities: Verify & Rebuild Data.

In the sidebar, System Administration holds three groups:

  • Setup: User Accounts, User Role Configuration, Page Access Configuration, Effective Access (View as User), Company, Security & Closing Date, Utilities: Verify & Rebuild Data, and Multi-Company Setup.
  • Utilities: Import from CSV and Backup & Restore.
  • Reports: the audit reports, under the heading Audit Reports. They are also listed in the Report Center.

How access works

Every page of the program is protected. Before you create users, it helps to know the pieces that decide what a user can do.

  • Roles. A role is a named set of permissions that matches a job, such as A/R Clerk or Cashier. You set the permissions once on the role, then give the role to users. Changing a role changes the access of every user who has it. A user can have several roles: their permissions add together.
  • Page access. The View right decides whether a page appears in the menu and can be opened at all. A denied page is hidden from the menu, the Home screen, the search box and report drill-downs. If someone opens it another way (an old bookmark, a link in an e-mail), the program shows Access denied and records the attempt in the audit trail.
  • Action rights. On each page a role may open, it may also be given Create, Edit, Delete/Void, Print/Export and Approve.
  • Sensitive permissions. Limits and yes/no rights that are not tied to one page, such as the largest discount a user may give without a supervisor.
  • Sites and POS terminals. Which inventory sites a user may record transactions for, and at which POS counters the user may sell.
  • User overrides. An exception for one user on one page, which always wins over the user's roles.
  • The Administrator role. A role marked Administrator (full access to everything) opens every page with every right and every sensitive permission.
  • Company Owner and Super Admin. When several companies run on the platform, the Company Owner and the Super Admin working inside a company have full authority in it, and no role or override can lock them out. See Company Owner and Super Admin.

The rights on a page

Right What it allows
View Open the page. It appears in the menu. Without View, the other rights on that page do nothing.
Create Add new records or transactions on the page, including Save & New and copying.
Edit Change saved records. On some setup pages it also runs the page's main action, such as saving settings or rebuilding data.
Delete/Void Delete or void records and transactions.

A user who has Approve on a page approves their own action there. Anyone else is stopped with a message that begins "Supervisor approval is required", and a supervisor who has Approve on that page types their own username and password in the Supervisor approval box of the same form. Nobody can approve their own transaction by typing their own name there.

Duties that should be kept apart

The program warns when one person would be able to handle both sides of a task that is normally split between two people. The pairs it checks are:

  • creating vendors and paying bills (Vendor Center and Pay Bills);
  • receiving customer payments and reconciling the bank account (Receive Payments and Reconcile);
  • adjusting inventory and counting it (Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand and Physical Inventory Count);
  • entering bills and writing checks (Enter Bills and Write Checks);
  • selling at the POS and depositing the cash (POS Register and Make Deposits).

A pair counts only when both pages are allowed with Create or Edit. When you save a role or a user that combines such duties, the save stops with a segregation of duties warning. You may still save by ticking the box under the warning; the decision is recorded in the audit trail as a Sod Override entry.

A user who works in two companies

When several companies share the platform, one person can work in two (or more) of them with one log-in, without being made a Super Admin: a group accountant who keeps the books of two companies, a purchasing officer who buys for both. The person stays a user of their own company. The other company gives them access with Add user from another company on its User Accounts page (the button is also on Employees & Users), and decides there what they may do.

Field What to enter
Their username The username the person logs in with at their own company. They keep that one log-in and password.
Roles in this company Tick at least one. They decide what the person may do in this company only.
Inventory sites, POS terminals As for any user: tick to limit the person to them.
Reason (recorded in the audit trail) Why the person gets access, for example "Accountant of the group".
Step by step: let a user of another company work here
  1. Log in to the company the person should also work in (with an account that may add users), and go to System Administration > User Accounts.
  2. Click Add user from another company.
  3. Type Their username, as they log in at their own company.
  4. Tick their Roles in this company, and sites or POS terminals if they should be limited.
  5. Type the reason and click Give access to this company.

The person's page opens. The person now sees the company name at the top of the screen as a switch with both companies, and moves between them without logging in again (see Working in two companies).

Example

Ana Cruz (accountant) is the accountant of Sample Trading Company and also keeps the books of ABC Distribution Corp. John Doe, the owner of ABC, opens User Accounts in ABC, clicks Add user from another company, types accountant, ticks the Accountant role and the reason "Group accountant", and clicks Give access to this company. Ana logs in at Sample Trading as always; the top bar now offers ABC Distribution Corp. too. In ABC she can do only what the Accountant role of ABC allows.

What the other company sees:

  • The person is listed in its User Accounts and Employees & Users with "User of" and their own company under the name. They have the same username and name, but no password here: there are no password fields and no Reset password for them. Their password is changed at their own company.
  • Their page has a User of another company box with their own company, when access was given and by whom, and when they last switched here.
  • What they do here is recorded in this company's audit trail under their username; each switch is recorded too ("Company Switch").
  • A supervisor approval they give here is checked against the password of their own company.

What their own company sees: their user page has an Also works in box with the other companies, and the list shows "Also works in" under their name.

Ending the access
  • In the company they work in: open the user and click Remove access to this company (with a reason). The account there is switched off, so their name stays on what they entered, and Give access back turns it on again later. The same is Remove access to this company under Manage on Employees & Users.
  • In their own company: Remove beside the other company in the Also works in box.
  • It also ends by itself when their account in their own company is deactivated or locked.

If the person is working in the other company when the access ends, the next page they open takes them back to their own company with the message "Your access to ... has ended. You are back in your own company."

Messages: "No active user of another company logs in with that username. Check the spelling with the person.", "... is already a user of this company. Change their roles in User Accounts instead.", "... already has access to this company.", "Give at least one role in this company.", "You cannot remove your own access."

Good to know

  • Users are never deleted, so their names stay on the transactions and audit entries they created. Deactivate them instead.
  • An account is locked after the number of wrong passwords set under Failed logins before lockout on Company, Security & Closing Date. The user sees "Too many failed attempts. The account is now locked." and then "This account is locked. Only an administrator can unlock it." An inactive user sees "This account is inactive. Contact your administrator."
  • You cannot deactivate your own account, remove the Administrator role from your own account, or deactivate the last active administrator ("This is the last active administrator.").
  • With several companies: only the Company Owner (or the Super Admin) can change the Company Owner's account, and the Company Owner cannot be deactivated. A user who registered with the Company Code and is still Waiting for approval cannot be reactivated here; approve them under Company > Join Requests (see Company Owner).
  • Messages you may see when saving: "That username is taken.", "That username is already used on this platform. Choose another.", "Assign at least one role.", "Enter the full name." and "The temporary password must have ..." followed by the rules it missed.
  • Every save, reset, unlock, deactivation and reactivation is recorded in the audit trail, with the reason you typed.

  • Every role change is recorded in the audit trail with the rights before and after, so an auditor can see who widened a role and when.

Page Access Configuration

This page decides which pages each role or user can open, without the detail of the action rights. It is the quickest place to allow or deny a whole page or module, to give one user an exception, and to compare all roles side by side.

Where to find it: System Administration > Page Access Configuration. The Page access view button on a role also opens it.

The page has three tabs: By role, By user (overrides) and Matrix (all roles). At the top, Effective access opens Effective Access (View as User) and Role permissions opens User Role Configuration.

By role

Choose a role in the drop-down. The pages are listed by module. The heading of each module shows how many of its pages are allowed (for example "Home 7 / 7 allowed"), and modules with at least one allowed page open by themselves. Each page shows its kind (center, list, transaction screen, setup screen or report, plus the report group) and a drop-down set to Allowed or Denied.

  1. Choose the role.
  2. Set each page to Allowed or Denied, or use Allow module and Deny module on a module's heading.
  3. Type a Reason for the change (recorded in the audit trail).
  4. Click Save page access.

The message tells you how many page settings changed, for example "2 page setting(s) changed for A/P Clerk. Changes apply the next time users open a page."

Note

A page you allow here gets only View, plus Print/Export if the role never had the page before. Set Create, Edit, Delete/Void, Approve (and Print/Export where it is missing) on User Role Configuration. Denying a page removes all its rights from the role.

An administrator role cannot be changed here: the page shows "The Administrator role has full access to every page." and the drop-downs are greyed out.

By user (overrides)

An override is an exception for one user on one page. It always wins over the user's roles, including the Administrator role. Use it for a short-term need or a single exception, so you do not have to create a new role.

Choose the user in the drop-down (you can type part of the name). A line shows the user's roles and how many overrides they have. For each page you see:

Column What it shows
Page The page name. Rows with an override are highlighted.
From roles Allowed (with the roles that allow it) or Denied, as the roles alone decide.
Setting for this user Inherit from role, Override: allow or Override: deny.
Actions when allowed by override Tick boxes C (Create), E (Edit), D (Delete/Void), P (Print/Export) and A (Approve), used only with Override: allow.
Set by Who set the override, when, and the reason.
  1. Choose the user.
  2. Open the module and find the page.
  3. Choose Override: allow and tick the actions, or Override: deny.
  4. Type a Reason for the override.
  5. Click Save overrides. Check effective access beside it shows the result.

To remove an override, set the page back to Inherit from role and save.

Example

In the demo company, Ben Garcia (arclerk) has Override: deny on Assess Finance Charges with the reason "Finance charges are assessed by the accountant", and Maria Santos (salesrep) has Override: allow with P on Customer Balance Summary because she "Needs balances for account visits".

You cannot deny yourself User Accounts, User Role Configuration or Page Access Configuration ("You cannot deny yourself access to the administration pages."), so you cannot lock yourself out by mistake.

Matrix (all roles)

The matrix shows every page down the side and every role across the top, with Allowed in green or Denied in red. Administrator roles show Allowed everywhere. The matrix shows page access only, not the action rights, and it cannot be changed here.

The links above it are Key pages only (eleven sensitive pages such as Chart of Accounts, Receive Payments, Pay Bills, Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand, POS Register, Reconcile, Audit Trail and User Role Configuration), All pages and Print.

Good to know

  • Saving on this page needs Edit on it. Without it, you can look but the drop-downs are greyed out.
  • Every change is recorded in the audit trail with each page's old and new setting and your reason. The User Access and Permission Changes report lists them.
  • The Company Owner and the Super Admin are never locked out by an override.

Effective Access (View as User)

This page shows what one user can really do once all their roles, overrides and sensitive permissions are combined. Use it to answer "why can't Ella open this page?" or to check an account before an audit.

Where to find it: System Administration > Effective Access (View as User). You can also click Effective access on a user's page, Check effective access on the By user tab of Page Access Configuration, or a figure in the Pages allowed column of the User Access and Permission Changes report.

Choose the user in the drop-down. Tick Show denied pages too to list every page, not only the allowed ones. Print prints the page.

What the page shows

  • The user box: the name, username and status, Roles, Sites ("all", or the site names), POS terminals ("any at allowed sites" when none is assigned) and Pages allowed (for example 4 of 168).
  • Sensitive permissions: each of the nine permissions with Yes, No, a number or No limit.
  • The page table: Module, Page, Access (Allowed or Denied), a tick under each right the user has (Create, Edit, Delete/Void, Print/Export, Approve), and Comes from.

Comes from names the role or roles that grant the page, "User override (allowed)" or "User override (denied)", "Administrator role", "Company Owner" or "Super Admin", or "No role grants it" for a denied page. Rows with an override are highlighted.

Good to know

  • Looking up a user is itself recorded in the audit trail (action View Access).
  • To change what you see here, change the user's roles on User Accounts, the roles on User Role Configuration, or the overrides on Page Access Configuration.

Run the checks at every month-end before you close the books.

Rebuild data

Option What it does
Recalculate inventory costs Replays every inventory transaction in date order: by date; on one date, first the transactions that only bring stock in (bills, item receipts, returns, adjustments up), then those that take some out and put some in (processing orders, builds, transfers), then those that only take it out (sales, adjustments down); then in the order entered. Step by step: rebuild data

  1. Make a backup on Backup & Restore.
  2. Ask everyone else to stop entering transactions until you finish.
  3. Tick one or both options.
  4. Type a Reason (kept in the audit trail), for example "month-end check".
  5. Click Rebuild data and confirm ("Rebuild now? Cost of sales on past sales may change. Make a backup first.").
  6. Read the Rebuild result, then look at the checks again.

The Rebuild result says how many inventory transactions were replayed over how many items, how many document balances were refreshed and how long it took. Users who may view costs also see the total cost of sales before and after, the difference, and a table of the first 100 transactions whose cost changed (Date, Type, Number, Cost before, Cost after, Difference). What it changes

A cost rebuild changes the amounts posted to the cost of sales account (5000 Cost of Goods Sold) and the inventory account (1200 Inventory) on transactions that move stock. Transactions dated on or before the closing date are re-costed as well; their sales totals do not change. The rebuild runs as one piece of work: if it fails part-way, nothing is changed. It is recorded in the audit trail as Rebuild Data, with the cost of sales before and after.

Good to know

  • Anyone who can open the page can run the checks. Rebuild data needs Edit on this page; without it the box says "You can run the checks above. Rebuilding data needs the edit permission on this page." The standard Accountant role can run the checks but not rebuild.
  • A rebuild re-posts only the transactions that move inventory. If General ledger balances or Every transaction balances still fails after a rebuild, contact support.
  • The page does not change any transaction's date, quantity or selling price.

Import from CSV

Use this page to load records from a spreadsheet instead of typing them: the chart of accounts, inventory items with their opening stock, customers, vendors, fixed assets, and whole transactions such as invoices, bills, checks, journal entries and POS sales. It is most useful when you start using the program and bring over lists and balances from another system.

Where to find it: System Administration > Import from CSV. The page has a Backup & Restore button at the top, because you should back up before every import.

How an import is safe

  • Nothing is written until every line of the file has been read and checked. A file with even one bad line changes nothing.
  • Check the file reads the whole file and reports every problem without writing anything.
  • The writing runs as one piece of work, so a problem found while writing also leaves the books exactly as they were.
  • A document number that is already in the books stops a transaction import, so the same file is not posted twice by mistake (checks are recognised by their check number).

Preparing the file

  • Save the sheet as CSV in Excel: File > Save As > CSV (Comma delimited). The file name must end in .csv or .txt. A file that uses semicolons instead of commas is read as well. Any of the ways Excel saves a CSV is read correctly: CSV (Comma delimited) in Windows' own encoding, CSV UTF-8 and Unicode Text (UTF-16, with tabs). Curly quotes (“Mismo” brand), dashes, the peso sign and accented letters in a name or a description come through as typed; they used to stop the import with an "Incorrect string value" error.
  • The first line holds the column names, such as code, name and opening_qty. The order does not matter, capitals do not matter, and a space may stand for the underscore (Sales Price is read as sales_price). A column you do not need can be left out. A column the program does not know is ignored. Blank lines are skipped.
  • Dates may be written as 2026-09-30, 09/30/2026, Sep 30, 2026 and in other common ways. A date such as 03/04/2026 is read month first, as March 4.
  • Numbers may have comma separators (12,500.00). Do not type a peso sign or other text in a number column.
  • Yes/no columns accept Yes, Y, 1, True, Oo or Opo for yes. Anything else counts as no.
  • A column that names a list entry (a term, a tax code, an account, a site, a customer) must match the list exactly. Accounts may be given by code or name (6040 or Office Supplies Expense), tax codes by code (VAT12), sites by code or name (WH or Main Warehouse), items by code, display name or barcode.
  • The template's second line is an example. Replace it with your own data or delete it before importing.

Step by step: check a file, then import it

  1. Choose the kind of record in What are you importing? The page shows a note on how that kind is read, a Download the blank template button, and on the right a table of its columns with the ones that are needed.
  2. Click Download the blank template and open it in Excel.
  3. Fill in one line per record (or per document line). Save it as CSV.
  4. Under CSV file, click Choose File and pick the file.
  5. Click Check the file.
  6. If the result says The file was not imported, correct the listed lines in the spreadsheet, save, choose the file again and check again.
  7. When the result says The file was checked and "The file is in order: nothing was written yet", look the lines over.
  8. Click Import these N item(s) (or Import these N record(s)) and confirm. The file you checked is imported without being chosen again.

Check and import in one step checks the file and, if it is in order, imports it at once. The program asks you to confirm ("Check and import this file in one step? Records are added to the books."). Use it only for files you have checked before.

The result of a check

When the file has problems, the result lists them by line number of the file (line 1 is the column names), up to 200 of them. Nothing was written.

A file that passed the check is kept on the server for one day for the Import these button. If it is gone ("The checked file is no longer here. Choose the file and check it again."), check the file again.

Inventory items: how the import treats them

  • An item whose code is already on the Item List is updated, not added again, and only the columns that are in the file change. Backup & Restore

A backup is one .sql file that holds every table and every row of the company's database. Make one before every month-end close, before a large price change, before every import and before a rebuild, and keep copies off the server. Restoring replaces what is in the database now with what is in the file.

Where to find it: System Administration > Backup & Restore. The Backup & Restore button on Import from CSV also opens it.

Make a backup

The box shows the Database (in the demo, gsmart; for a company added later on a multi-company platform, the company's name), the number of Tables, the Size on disk and the Backups kept here.

Field What to choose
What is this backup for? (optional) A note, such as "Before the month-end close". It is kept in the backup history and the audit trail.
What to save Everything: structure and data for a real backup, or Structure only (no rows) for empty tables.
Backups to keep on the server Keep them all, The newest 5, The newest 10 (the standard) or The newest 20. After the backup, older files beyond this number are deleted from the server.
Compress the file (.sql.gz, much smaller) Ticked: the file is compressed. Shown only when the server can compress.
  1. Type what the backup is for.
  2. Leave What to save on Everything: structure and data.
  3. Choose how many backups to keep.
  4. Click Make the backup now and leave the page open until it finishes.

The message says "Backup written:" with the file name and size, and how many older backups were removed. The file is named after the database and the date and time, for example gsmart_20260927_141929.sql.gz. A structure-only backup ends in _structure.

Backups on the server

The table lists the files in the backups folder (shown beside the heading, for example storage/backups), newest first, with File, Made and Size.

  • Download saves a copy to your computer. Do this at least weekly and keep the copy somewhere other than the server.
  • The trash button deletes a file. In the box that opens, type the Reason (required, recorded in the audit trail) and click Delete this backup.
  • Files whose name starts with before_restore_ are the automatic backups made before a restore. Files marked uploaded came from From a file on my computer.

Backup history lists the last 20 backups, restores and deletions: When, What (Backup, Restore or Deleted), File, Tables, Rows, By and Note.

Restore from a backup

Important

A restore replaces every table of the database with what the file holds. Anything entered since that backup was made is lost. Ask everyone else to log out first.

  1. Open the tab From a backup kept here (or From a file on my computer to upload a .sql or .sql.gz file).
  2. Choose the Backup to restore (or choose the file).
  3. Type the Reason (recorded in the audit trail).
  4. Type RESTORE in Type RESTORE to confirm.
  5. Click Restore (or Upload and restore) and confirm.

The message says how many statements were run. If some could not be run, a card Statements that could not be run lists them; check them before working in the books again.

The upload tab tells you the largest file this server accepts. Copy a larger file into the backups folder instead and restore it from From a backup kept here.

The safeguards

  • Before it changes anything, the restore makes a fresh backup of what is there now (before_restore_...), so a restore can itself be undone by restoring that file.
  • You must type RESTORE; otherwise "Type RESTORE in the box to confirm. Nothing has been changed."
  • Only an administrator can restore (a user with the Administrator role, the Company Owner or the Super Admin), and only with Edit on this page. Others see "Only an administrator can restore the database."
  • After the restore, the audit trail's protection is put back in place. With several companies, the list of users on the platform is brought in line with the restored users; a username that belongs to another company is reported, and that user cannot log in until the username is changed.
  • If more than 25 statements fail, the restore stops.

What is refused

  • A file that does not end in .sql or .sql.gz ("A backup file ends in .sql or .sql.gz.").
  • A single statement larger than 32 MB. Restore such a file with phpMyAdmin or Navicat.
  • With several companies, only statements that rebuild the company's own tables are run (such as CREATE TABLE, DROP TABLE, INSERT INTO and SET). A statement that changes server settings or passwords, creates something other than a table, reads other data or names another database is listed as "not run".

Good to know

  • Making a backup needs Create, downloading needs Print/Export, and deleting needs Delete/Void on this page.
  • The program does not make backups on a schedule. Make them yourself at the times listed above.
  • A restore also brings back the audit trail and the backup history as they were in the file. Entries made after that backup remain in the automatic before_restore_ file.
  • Backups to keep on the server also counts the before_restore_ and uploaded files, so a low number may remove them. Download any file you want to keep.
  • A backup made here can be read back with phpMyAdmin (Import) or Navicat (Execute SQL File), and a file those programs wrote can be restored here. Compressed files are read back here directly.
  • The backup file holds the tables and rows but not the two triggers that protect the audit trail. A restore on this page puts them back by itself. If you read a backup back with phpMyAdmin or Navicat instead, also run the program's database/triggers.sql file the same way, so the audit trail is protected again.
  • If the backups folder cannot be written to, the page says so and Make the backup now is greyed out. Ask whoever manages the server to give the web server write permission on the storage folder.
  • Backups, restores, downloads and deletions are all recorded in the audit trail.

  • Messages you may see: "Tick the box to confirm that a backup of the database was made.", "The two Super Admin passwords do not match.", "The Super Admin needs a username of its own: admin is already a user of this company.", "Choose an active user of this company as its Company Owner." and "The master database gsmart_master already belongs to other companies."

Audit reports

The audit trail is a permanent log of every action that creates, changes or removes information, and of every log-in, failed log-in and denied page. The audit reports read it in different ways.

What is recorded. Each entry holds the date and time, the user and their roles at the time, the computer's IP address and browser, the POS terminal and site where there is one, the module and page, the action, the record (with its number), the amount, the fields that changed (before and after), a full copy of the record where useful, the reason typed and the supervisor who approved. Among the actions recorded are: create, edit, delete and void of every transaction; print, reprint, export and e-mail of documents and reports; log-in, log-out, session timeout, failed log-ins, account locks and unlocks, password changes and resets; denied pages and failed supervisor approvals; changes to users, roles, page access, overrides, settings and the closing date; segregation of duties and credit limit overrides; imports, backups, restores and data rebuilds; POS shifts, cash drops and readings; bank reconciliations; memorized transactions entered automatically.

It cannot be changed. Nobody, not even an administrator, can edit or delete an audit entry. Two database triggers refuse any change or deletion, even when someone works on the database directly with another program. The audit trail cannot be switched off. The only way older entries are replaced is by restoring a backup, which brings back the audit trail as it was in the backup file.

The last five reports below (Voided and Deleted Transactions Summary and Detail, User Activity, Login History, and User Access and Permission Changes) use the common report tools (dates, Refresh, Columns, Print / PDF, Export to spreadsheet, Email link, Modify Report, Memorize and drill-down), explained in Working with reports.

Transaction History

Transaction History shows every recorded version of one transaction, oldest first: who created it, each edit with the fields that changed, prints, voids and deletions, with the reasons and approvals. It works even for a transaction that was deleted, because the audit trail kept a copy.

Where to find it: System Administration > Reports > Transaction History. You can also click History on a transaction form, the clock icon beside a transaction in the Audit Trail, or a number in the Voided and Deleted Transactions Summary.

  1. Type the document number, for example INV-00012, in the box.
  2. Click Find.

The program opens the history of the latest transaction whose name contains what you typed, so type the whole number.

The heading shows the transaction and its present status (such as Partial, Paid or Void), or deleted. Open current version at the top opens the transaction itself. Each entry is a card with:

  • the action and, for saves, the version number ("Create, version 1"), the user and their roles, the date and time and the IP address;
  • the Reason and Approved by, when there are any;
  • a Field / Before / After table of what changed;
  • Content after this action (or Content before this action for a void or delete), which opens the full header and lines as they were.

A card for a void or a deletion has a red border.

Good to know

Someone who may not open the transaction's own page, and may not open the Audit Trail either, is refused the history of that transaction.

The same transactions as the summary, with the full content of each as it was just before it was voided or deleted: its date, name, memo and total, every line, and the documents it had been applied to.

Where to find it: System Administration > Reports > Voided and Deleted Transactions Detail, or Home > Report Center.

These appendices collect the reference material you look up rather than read from start to finish: what every kind of transaction does to the books, what the installed roles can do, how document numbers work, what the program's messages mean, what the system administrator needs to know, and a glossary.

Appendix A. What each transaction records

Every transaction you save is turned into general ledger lines and, for stocked items, into inventory movements. The rules are fixed in the program: you choose the accounts (through Module Default Accounts, items, tax codes and asset categories), but you never type the posting of an invoice or a bill yourself. This appendix lists, for every kind of transaction, the accounts it debits and credits, what it does to stock and to customer or vendor balances, and the page where you enter it.

How to read this appendix

  • The account names are those of the standard chart of accounts of a contractor delivered with the program. Your company posts to the accounts chosen under Module Default Accounts, on each item in the Item List, on each tax code in Terms, Tax Codes & Other Lists and on each asset category.
  • Prices and amounts on sales and purchase forms are VAT-exclusive. VAT is worked out on each line and posted to the account of the line's tax code (in the demo, 2100 Output Tax on sales and 1300 Input Tax on purchases).
  • Stocked items are items of the types Inventory part and Inventory assembly. Only they have a quantity on hand and a value. Non-inventory parts, services and other charges post to accounts only.
  • A stocked item leaves stock at its weighted-average cost: the book value of the item divided by the quantity on hand, over all sites. A sale that brings the quantity to zero takes all the value that is left.
  • A customer balance is what 1100 Accounts Receivable holds for that customer and its jobs. A vendor balance is what 2000 Accounts Payable holds for that vendor.
  • Every ledger line carries the class of the transaction (see The class on a transaction, at the end of this appendix).
Tip

To see the entry of any saved transaction, open it. The box General ledger entries created automatically lists every line with its account, memo, class, debit and credit. On sales documents (invoices, sales receipts, statement and finance charges, credit memos, POS sales and returns) the box is shown only to users who have the special permission View costs and margins, because it shows the cost of goods sold.

Sales and receivables

Estimate and Sales Order

Entered on Create Estimates and Create Sales Orders.

  • Ledger: nothing. The view of the document says Non-posting document: it records a commitment and does not affect the ledger.
  • Stock: nothing moves. The quantities of an open sales order that are not yet invoiced count as on sales order in the stock status reports and the Inventory Center.
  • Balances: no effect on the customer's balance.
  • Status: an estimate is Pending, becomes Accepted when it is turned into a sales order or partly invoiced, and Closed when it is fully invoiced or closed with Close. A sales order is Open, then Partially filled, then Closed when every line is invoiced or closed.

Invoice

Entered on Create Invoices. Invoices are also made by Create Batch Invoices and by a charge sale on the POS Register.

Account Debit Credit
1100 Accounts Receivable Invoice total, VAT included
Income account of each item (for example 4000 Sales, 4010 Service Income) Line amount
2100 Output Tax (the tax code's account) VAT of the lines
5000 Cost of Goods Sold (the item's cost account) Average cost of stocked items
1200 Inventory (the item's asset account) Average cost of stocked items
  • Stock: stocked items leave the Inventory site of the invoice (or the site of the line) at their average cost. The cost is stored on the line and shows in the Cost column for users who may view costs.
  • Balances: the customer's balance goes up by the invoice total. The invoice stays open until payments or credits are applied to it.
  • Special lines: a discount item is posted as a debit to its own income account (4060 Sales Discounts for DISC-10 and DISC-AMT); a payment item such as DOWNPMT (Down Payment Received) debits 1050 Undeposited Funds and lowers the amount charged to Accounts Receivable; billable costs added from bills and checks credit 4110 Reimbursable Expense Income; a subtotal line posts nothing; a group item is entered as the separate items it holds.
  • Pending: an invoice saved with Pending (non-posting until marked final) ticked posts nothing (see Pending transactions, later in this appendix).
Example

ABC Trading Corp. is invoiced for one LAP-001 Laptop 14-inch i5 16GB at ₱39,990.00 plus 12% VAT, and the laptop's average cost is ₱32,000.00. The invoice debits 1100 Accounts Receivable ₱44,788.80, credits 4000 Sales ₱39,990.00 and 2100 Output Tax ₱4,798.80, and debits 5000 Cost of Goods Sold and credits 1200 Inventory ₱32,000.00. One laptop leaves the Main Warehouse, and ABC Trading Corp.'s balance goes up by ₱44,788.80.

Sales Receipt

Entered on Enter Sales Receipts.

  • Ledger: the same as an invoice, except that the total is debited to the Deposit to account instead of Accounts Receivable. The default is 1050 Undeposited Funds; you can also choose a bank account or another current asset.
  • Stock: stocked items leave the Inventory site at average cost.
  • Balances: no effect on the customer's balance: the sale is paid on the spot.
  • Later: a sales receipt deposited to 1050 Undeposited Funds waits in Make Deposits until you deposit it.

Statement Charge

Entered on Enter Statement Charges.

  • Ledger: debit 1100 Accounts Receivable, credit the income account of each item (for example 4100 Finance Charge Income for LATE-FEE Late Payment Fee, 4020 Delivery Income for DEL-FEE Delivery Fee), and credit Output Tax for any VAT.
  • Stock: statement charges are meant for services and other charges. A stocked item on one would leave stock like on an invoice.
  • Balances: the customer's balance goes up. The charge appears on the next statement and can be paid in Receive Payments.

Finance Charge

Made on Assess Finance Charges, one for each customer you select.

  • Ledger: debit 1100 Accounts Receivable, credit 4100 Finance Charge Income. No VAT.
  • Stock: none.
  • Balances: the customer's balance goes up. The charge is due on the assessment date.

Customer Payment

Entered on Receive Payments, or as a payment at the counter on the POS Register.

Account Debit Credit
Deposit to account (default 1050 Undeposited Funds) Amount received
4060 Sales Discounts Early-payment discount
1310 Creditable Withholding Tax (or the tax's own account) Income tax withheld by the customer (1% / 2%)
1315 Creditable VAT Withheld (or the tax's own account) VAT withheld by the customer (5%)
1100 Accounts Receivable Amount received plus discount plus withholding
  • Stock: none.
  • Balances: the customer's balance goes down by the amount received plus the discount and withholding. The invoices the payment is applied to become Partially paid or Paid. An amount received but not applied stays on the payment as an unapplied credit.
  • Taxes withheld: each type ticked in the Withholding box is kept with its base, rate and ATC; the 1% / 2% and the 5% go to different accounts, as the BIR requires (an income-tax credit, and a VAT credit for 2550Q Item 16). A payment saved before the types were kept posts its withholding to 1310.
  • Applying credits: using an existing credit memo or unapplied payment on the same screen posts nothing new; it only records which invoice the credit pays.
  • At the POS: a payment taken at the counter debits the terminal's cash account for cash, or the clearing account of the payment method, and is applied to the customer's oldest open invoices first.
Example

ABC Trading Corp. pays the ₱44,788.80 invoice by check and withholds 1% of the VAT-exclusive amount, ₱399.90. You enter Amount received ₱44,388.90, and on the invoice line Withholding ₱399.90 and Payment ₱44,388.90. The payment debits 1050 Undeposited Funds ₱44,388.90 and 1310 Creditable Withholding Tax ₱399.90, and credits 1100 Accounts Receivable ₱44,788.80. The invoice is paid in full.

Credit Memo

Entered on Create Credit Memos/Refunds.

Account Debit Credit
4050 Sales Returns and Allowances Line amounts
2100 Output Tax VAT of the lines
1100 Accounts Receivable Credit memo total
1200 Inventory Average cost of goods returned to stock
5000 Cost of Goods Sold Average cost of goods returned to stock
  • Stock: only when Return goods to stock is ticked: stocked items come back into the Return to site (or the inventory site of the credit memo) at the item's current average cost. Without the tick, only the first three lines are posted.
  • Balances: the customer's balance goes down. What happens to the credit depends on the choice under What to do with this credit: keep it as an available credit, apply it to an invoice now, or give a refund.

Customer Refund

Recorded with the Refund button of a credit memo or of a payment that has an unapplied credit, on Create Credit Memos/Refunds.

  • Ledger: debit 1100 Accounts Receivable, credit the Pay from account (a bank or cash account).
  • Stock: none.
  • Balances: the customer's balance goes back up by the refund, which uses up the credit.
  • Rules: the refund cannot be more than the available credit. A refund cannot be edited: void it and record a new one. A refund above your amount limit needs supervisor approval.

Purchases and payables

Purchase Order

Entered on Create Purchase Orders.

  • Ledger: nothing. It is a non-posting document.
  • Stock: nothing moves. The quantities not yet received count as on purchase order in the stock status reports.
  • Balances: no effect on the vendor's balance.
  • Status: Open, Partially filled as items are received, Closed when every line is received or closed.

Item Receipt

Entered on Receive Items, when goods arrive before the vendor's bill.

  • Ledger: debit 1200 Inventory (the item's asset account) for stocked items, or the item's expense account for other items, and credit 2000 Accounts Payable. An item receipt carries no VAT.
  • Stock: stocked items come into the receiving site at the cost on the receipt, and the average cost is worked out again.
  • Balances: the vendor's balance goes up at once: open item receipts are part of Accounts Payable and of the A/P aging.
  • Editing: an item receipt not billed yet can be edited; it is posted again (stock, Inventory and Accounts Payable) and the purchase order follows the new quantities received.
  • Later: Enter Bill for Received Items turns the same record into a bill with a new BILL- number. The quantities stay as received; the cost and the VAT can change, and inventory value and the payable are adjusted to the billed cost.

Bill

Entered on Enter Bills, Receive Items and Enter Bills or Enter Bill for Received Items. Receipt Management, the Fixed Asset Register and Asset Maintenance can also create bills.

Account Debit Credit
Account of each line on the Expenses tab Line amount
1200 Inventory, for stocked items on the Items tab Line amount
Expense account of other items (else their cost account, else 6110) Line amount
1300 Input Tax VAT of the lines
2000 Accounts Payable Bill total
  • Stock: stocked items come into the site of the line (or the Default site for items) at the bill's cost, and the item's average cost is worked out again.
  • Balances: the vendor's balance goes up. The bill stays open until it is paid in Pay Bills or a debit memo is applied to it.
  • Billable costs: a line marked billable to a Customer:Job is offered on that customer's next invoice.
Example

TechDistributors Inc. bills 5 MOU-001 Wireless Mouse at ₱350.00. The bill debits 1200 Inventory ₱1,750.00 and 1300 Input Tax ₱210.00, and credits 2000 Accounts Payable ₱1,960.00. Five mice come into stock, and the vendor's balance goes up by ₱1,960.00.

Supplier Debit Memo

Entered on Supplier Debit Memos, for goods returned to a supplier, price or billing corrections and allowances.

  • Ledger: debit 2000 Accounts Payable for the total; credit the account of each expense line, 1200 Inventory for stocked items returned, and 1300 Input Tax for the VAT.
  • Stock: stocked items leave the site of the line at the amount on the debit memo.
  • Balances: the vendor's balance goes down. The available amount is applied to the supplier's bills on the debit memo's own page or under Credits in Pay Bills; applying it posts nothing further.

Bill Payment

Made on Pay Bills. One bill payment is recorded for each vendor.

Account Debit Credit
2000 Accounts Payable Payment plus discount plus taxes withheld
Payment account (bank or credit card, default 1010 Cash in Bank - BDO Checking) Payment
5050 Purchase Discounts Discount taken
2110 Withholding Tax Payable (or the tax's own account) Income tax withheld (1% / 2%)
2115 VAT Withheld Payable (or the tax's own account) VAT withheld (5%)

  • Balances: the vendor's balance goes down, and the bills paid become Partially paid or Paid. Supplier credits used on the same screen only link the credit to the bill.
  • Rules: a bill payment can be edited (date, account, check number, the bills it pays, the amounts and the taxes withheld); the vendor stays the same. A payment above your amount limit needs supervisor approval.
  • Taxes withheld: each type ticked in the Withholding column is kept with the bill, its base (net of VAT), rate and ATC, for BIR Form 2307 and the BIR Reports.

a ₱112,000.00 bill (₱100,000.00 plus VAT) paid less 1% and 5% withheld: debit 2000 Accounts Payable ₱112,000.00; credit 1010 Cash in Bank ₱106,000.00, 2110 Withholding Tax Payable ₱1,000.00 and 2115 VAT Withheld Payable ₱5,000.00.

Advance to Supplier

Entered on Advances to Suppliers.

Account Debit Credit
Advance account (default 1410 Advances to Suppliers) Amount paid
Paid from (bank or credit card) Amount paid
  • Balances: not part of Accounts Payable: the vendor's balance and the A/P aging do not change until the advance is applied to a bill in Pay Bills.
  • Rules: it can be edited, but not below what was applied; it is voided only after its applications are voided.

Check

Entered on Write Checks or as a quick entry in Use Register. Receipt Management, the Fixed Asset Register and Asset Maintenance can also write checks.

  • Ledger: debit the lines exactly as on a bill (expense accounts, 1200 Inventory for stocked items, the expense account of other items) and 1300 Input Tax; credit the Bank / credit card account the check is drawn on. A "check" drawn on 2050 Company Credit Card records a card charge.
  • Stock: stocked items on the Items tab come into stock like on a bill.
  • Balances: a check does not go through Accounts Payable, so the vendor's balance does not change. Pay open bills with Pay Bills instead.
  • Rules: a check above your amount limit needs supervisor approval. Lines to accounts marked With SL need a name, or a payee on the check.

Cash Voucher

Entered on Cash Voucher, for petty cash, revolving fund and other cash payments. Asset Maintenance can also make one.

  • Ledger: the account lines exactly as entered (debit or credit), plus one credit to the Cash account (for example 1005 Petty Cash Fund) for the cash amount. The debits less the credits of the lines must equal the cash amount.
  • Stock: none.
  • Balances: a line to 1100 Accounts Receivable must name a customer and changes that customer's balance; a line to 2000 Accounts Payable must name a supplier and changes that supplier's balance.

Banking

Deposit

Transfer

Entered on Transfer Funds or as a quick entry in Use Register.

  • Ledger: debit the Transfer funds to account, credit the Transfer funds from account. It does not touch income or expenses.
  • Stock and balances: none.

Service Charge and Interest Earned

Recorded by Reconcile when you finish a reconciliation with a Service charge or Interest earned amount.

  • Service Charge: debit the account chosen beside it (default 6080 Bank and Merchant Charges), credit the bank account.
  • Interest Earned: debit the bank account, credit the account chosen beside it (default 4200 Interest Income).
  • Both are dated on the dates you enter beside them, take their numbers from the JE- series, and are marked cleared in the reconciliation straight away.
  • A reconciliation saved for later keeps the two amounts but posts nothing. Undo last voids the two entries and keeps their amounts on the reopened reconciliation, so they are posted once more when it is finished.

General ledger

Journal Entry

Entered on Make Journal Entries.

  • Ledger: every line exactly as entered, debit or credit. The debits must equal the credits, and an entry needs at least two lines.
  • Names: a line to 1100 Accounts Receivable needs a customer and changes that customer's balance; a line to 2000 Accounts Payable needs a vendor and changes that vendor's balance; a line to an account marked With SL needs a customer, supplier, employee or other name.
  • Stock: none. A journal entry to 1200 Inventory changes the ledger but not the quantities or values of items, so the inventory valuation reports will no longer agree with the ledger. Use Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand for stock.

Opening Balance

Entered on Make Journal Entries with Opening balance entry ticked. It posts like a journal entry and is listed with the journal entries.

  • The Fixed Asset Register also makes one when a new asset is saved with Record the purchase set to Existing asset (opening balance): debit the category's asset account for the cost, credit 3900 Opening Balance Equity for the cost less the opening accumulated depreciation, and credit the accumulated depreciation account for the opening accumulated depreciation. Import from CSV does the same for imported fixed assets.
  • Opening stock is not an opening balance entry: it is an inventory adjustment against 3900 Opening Balance Equity (see below).
Tip

For customers' and vendors' opening balances, enter one line per customer to 1100 Accounts Receivable (or per vendor to 2000 Accounts Payable) with the name on the line, balanced against 3900 Opening Balance Equity. The names are required on those lines.

Inventory

Inventory Adjustment

Entered on Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand. Physical Inventory Count posts its differences as an inventory adjustment, and the Item List posts one when a new stocked item is saved with a Quantity on hand.

  • Quantity added: debit 1200 Inventory, credit the Adjustment account (default 5100 Inventory Adjustments and Shrinkage), at the item's average cost.
  • Quantity removed: debit the adjustment account, credit 1200 Inventory, at the item's average cost.
  • Value changed (Total value): debit or credit 1200 Inventory against the adjustment account for the difference, with no change in quantity. Only users who may view costs can adjust values.
  • Opening quantity from the Item List: debit 1200 Inventory for the Total value entered, credit 3900 Opening Balance Equity.
  • Stock: the quantity changes at the site of the adjustment, and the average cost is worked out again.
  • Editing: an adjustment can be edited; it is taken off the books, worked out again against the stock without it, and posted again under the same number.

Inventory Transfer

Entered on Transfer Inventory.

  • Ledger: nothing, because both sites use the same inventory account. The page confirms Quantities moved without changing total inventory value.
  • Stock: the quantity leaves the from site at the item's average cost and arrives at the to site with the same value.
  • Editing: a transfer can be edited (date, sites, lines); the stock moves again.

Fixed assets

Depreciation

Posted by Run Depreciation, dated the last day of the month.

  • Ledger: for each asset category and class, debit the category's depreciation expense account (6090 Depreciation Expense in the demo) and credit its accumulated depreciation account (for example 1555 Accumulated Depreciation - Computer and POS).
  • Assets: the month is marked as run for each asset. An asset whose net book value reaches its salvage value becomes fully depreciated.
  • Undoing: voiding or deleting the entry frees the month, so it can be run again.

Asset Disposal

Posted by Dispose of Assets.

Asset purchases and maintenance

  • A new asset in the Fixed Asset Register is recorded as a check (Paid now (Write Checks)), a bill (On account (Enter Bills)) or an opening balance, each debiting the category's asset account; or nothing, with Already recorded elsewhere.
  • A record in Asset Maintenance is paid through a bill, a check or a cash voucher that debits the Parts charged to and Labor charged to accounts (proposed: the account whose name contains "Repair", 6030 Repairs and Maintenance in the demo) and, with VAT, 1300 Input Tax.

Point of Sale

POS Sale

Completed on the POS Register.

Account Debit Credit
Account of each tender Amount paid with it
Income account of each item Line amount
2100 Output Tax VAT of the lines
5000 Cost of Goods Sold Average cost of stocked items
1200 Inventory Average cost of stocked items

POS Return

Processed on POS Receipts, Returns & Voids, always with supervisor approval.

  • Ledger: debit 4050 Sales Returns and Allowances and 2100 Output Tax; credit the account of the refund method (cash: the terminal's cash account).
  • Stock: when Goods can be resold (back to store stock) is ticked, the items come back into the store's site; when it is not, they go to the damaged-goods site. Either way they come back at the current average cost: debit 1200 Inventory, credit 5000 Cost of Goods Sold. If no damaged-goods site exists, items that cannot be resold do not come back into stock.

Cash Over/Short

Posted by Shifts, Cash Drops & Readings when a register is closed and the cash counted differs from the expected cash.

  • Short: debit 6200 Cash Over and Short, credit the terminal's cash account.
  • Over: debit the terminal's cash account, credit 6200 Cash Over and Short.
  • Example: Makati Counter 1 is ₱50.00 short: 6200 Cash Over and Short is debited and 1001 Cash on Hand - Store Registers credited ₱50.00.

Voiding versus deleting

Both are done from the view of a saved transaction with the Void and Delete buttons. Both need the Delete/Void right on the page, a reason (Reason (required, recorded in the audit trail)), and a date after the closing date.

What happens Void Delete
The document Stays, marked VOID with the reason, date and user Is removed
Ledger and stock Everything it posted is taken out Everything it posted is taken out
Its number Stays in use Is not given out again
Available for Posting transactions only All, except POS sales, returns and over/short
Audit trail Full copy with the reason Full copy with the reason
  • A voided transaction cannot be edited or voided again. It still appears in lists and reports as void, and in Voided and Deleted Transactions Detail.
  • Estimates, sales orders and purchase orders cannot be voided (they post nothing); delete them, or use Close to stop expecting the rest.
  • Voiding or deleting is refused while:
  • payments or credits are applied to the invoice, statement charge, finance charge or bill (remove them first by editing the payment or credit);
  • the payment or sales receipt is included in a deposit (void the deposit first);
  • the credit memo or payment has been refunded (void the refund first);
  • the estimate, sales order or purchase order has been converted or received in part (close it instead).
  • Voiding or deleting a payment or credit releases the invoices it paid, which become open again.
  • Voiding or deleting a check, bill payment or customer refund above your amount limit, and voiding any POS sale or return, needs supervisor approval.
  • A transaction cleared in a bank reconciliation can be changed, voided or deleted only by a user with the closed-period permission. The change is listed on the Reconciliation Discrepancy report.
Note

Item receipts, inventory adjustments, inventory transfers, builds and finance charges cannot be changed after they are saved, so they have no Edit button. To correct one, void it and enter it again. Deposits and bill payments can be edited.

The closing date

The closing date protects the periods you have finished. It is set in Company, Security & Closing Date in the box Books closed through (blank = open).

Once a closing date is set, a user without the special permission Can change transactions on/before the closing date or already reconciled cannot:

  • save a transaction dated on or before the closing date;
  • edit, void or delete a transaction dated on or before it, or move it to a later date;
  • import transactions or opening stock dated on or before it.

The messages are The books are closed through 07/08/2026. You do not have permission to record transactions dated on or before the closing date. and This transaction is dated on or before the closing date (07/08/2026) and cannot be changed. Administrators, the Company Owner and the Super Admin always have the permission; of the installed roles, no other role has it.

  • A memorized transaction set to be entered automatically on a date in the closed period stops, and is set back to Remind me when it is due.
  • The same permission is needed to change a transaction cleared in a bank reconciliation, whatever its date.
Tip

After each month-end close, make a backup, check the reports, and then move the closing date to the last day of that month.

The class on a transaction

A class is your own way of splitting the books (branches, departments, projects). The classes are kept in the Class List.

  • Every transaction form has a Class box. The class is copied to every ledger line the transaction posts, so every financial report can be filtered by class and the "by Class" statements show one column per class.
  • On journal entries and cash vouchers a line can have its own class; a line without one takes the class at the top.
  • When you save a transaction without a class, a window No class selected warns that it will be saved as Unclassified. Choose Choose a class to go back, or Continue saving. The window does not appear while the Class List is empty.
  • On the POS Register a sale starts with the user's default class (set in Company, Security & Closing Date) or else the terminal's default class, and the class is kept for the next sale.
  • Run Depreciation posts each asset under its own default class when no class is chosen on the run.
  • A class already used on transactions cannot be deleted; make it inactive instead. Reports list entries without a class as (Unclassified).

How numbers are given

  • A number is taken when the transaction is first saved, never when the form is opened. The form shows the next number as a hint, for example INV-00024 (auto) in the Number box.
  • If saving fails (a message in red), no number is used up.
  • The counter only goes forward. A voided transaction keeps its number; a deleted one leaves a gap, and the number is not given out again. A suspended POS sale removed with Cancel suspended sale is deleted, so it also leaves a gap in the POS- series.
  • Because checks, bill payments and customer refunds share one series, a bill payment made by bank transfer or card also takes the next check number.
  • Changing a transaction's date does not change its number. When an item receipt is turned into a bill with Enter Bill for Received Items, the record gets the next BILL- number in place of its IR- number.
  • A new company created on the platform starts every series again from 1.

Typing your own number

On these forms you can type a number instead of taking the automatic one, both when entering and when editing:

  • Number on estimates, sales orders, invoices, sales receipts, statement charges, credit memos, purchase orders, bills and supplier debit memos;
  • Check no. on Write Checks;
  • Entry no. on Make Journal Entries;
  • CAV entry no. on Cash Voucher.

Leave the box blank to get the next number. A number you type does not move the counter.

Important

Only the cash voucher checks that a typed number is not used already (CAV entry no. ... is already used. Leave the number blank to get the next one.). On the other forms the program accepts a number that is already in use, and a typed number that is ahead of the counter will be given out again later. Type your own numbers only when you must match a pre-printed form, and check the list first.

All other transactions (payments, bill payments, deposits, transfers, item receipts, adjustments, transfers of stock, builds, processing orders, POS transactions and the entries the program makes) are always numbered automatically.

POS receipt numbers and Z-reading numbers

  • Every POS terminal has its own receipt series: the Receipt prefix set in POS Terminals followed by an 8-digit number, for example MK1-00000001 for Makati Counter 1 and CB1-00000001 for Cebu Counter 1. New terminals are offered the prefix OR-.
  • Sales, returns, charge sales and payments taken at the counter each take the next receipt number of the terminal, in addition to their own transaction number.
  • Z-readings are numbered per terminal (#1, #2 ...) when the register is closed.
  • The prefix can be changed on the POS Terminals page. The running number cannot be reset there (Receipt numbers are sequential per terminal and cannot be reset here.).

Can the series be changed?

Not from within the program. There is no page for the prefixes or the next numbers of the series; they are kept in the sequences table of the company database. A system administrator with access to the database (phpMyAdmin or Navicat) can change a prefix or the next number there, for example to start at the next number of your pre-printed invoices.

Important

Make a backup before changing the table, and never set a next number at or below a number already used: the program would give out duplicate numbers.

Other codes

  • Company Code: with several companies, each company has a code such as ABD-7K2QX (see Appendix F). It is not a document number and cannot be chosen; the Company Owner or the Super Admin can only regenerate it.
  • Codes you choose yourself: account codes, item codes, customer and vendor names, site codes and terminal codes are typed by you and must be unique.

Appendix E. For the system administrator

This appendix is for the person who installs and looks after the program. It needs access to the server, the database and the program folder.

Requirements

Component Requirement
PHP 8.1 or newer (8.2 or newer recommended), with the pdo_mysql and mbstring extensions
Database MySQL 5.5.3 or newer, or MariaDB 5.5 or newer
Web server Apache (the .htaccess files are included) or nginx
Browser Any current browser
Internet Only to load Bootstrap from a CDN; see Offline use

PHP and MySQL versions are separate: old XAMPP packages with MySQL 5.5 come with PHP 5, which cannot run this program. Install a current XAMPP, or point a PHP 8 installation at your MySQL 5.5 server.

Installing

With XAMPP and phpMyAdmin:

  1. Unzip the folder into C:\xampp\htdocs\, so that you have C:\xampp\htdocs\gsmart-construction\index.php.
  2. Start Apache and MySQL in the XAMPP Control Panel.
  3. Open phpMyAdmin at http://localhost/phpmyadmin and choose Import.
  4. Choose database/gsmart_construction_clean.sql to start real work (the set-up lists and the user accounts, no client, project or transaction), or database/gsmart_construction_sample.sql for the sample contractor with four projects and a year of work. Click Go. Either file creates the company database gsmart_construction and the platform database gsmart_construction_master.
  5. If your MySQL user has a password, or you used another database name, edit config/config.php.
  6. Open http://localhost/gsmart-construction/ and log in as admin with the password Admin@12345 (the other accounts are listed in README.md; the Super Admin is superadmin with Super@12345).

From the command line, php tools/install.php does the same (see Command-line tools).

Configuration: config/config.php

Setting What it holds
db: host, port, name, user, pass, charset The database connection (utf8mb4)
app: name The program name shown on screen; left as "Accounting System", the program shows GSMaRT Construction System
app: timezone The company time zone, Asia/Manila by default
app: currency The currency sign, ₱
app: debug Detailed error pages, switched on only by the environment variable APP_DEBUG=1
app: upload_dir Where uploaded receipts are kept (storage/receipts)
master: name (optional) Another name for the platform database when several companies are set up

The environment variables DB_HOST, DB_PORT, DB_NAME, DB_USER and DB_PASS override the values in the file.

Important

config/config.php holds the database user and password. Never send it to anyone, never attach it to a support e-mail or a screenshot, and never place a copy where the web server can serve it. The .htaccess files (or the nginx rules) stop browsers from opening the config, app, database, modules, storage, tools and views folders; keep them in place.

Settings that users change inside the program (company details, password rules, lockout, session timeout, finance charges, negative stock, the closing date) are in Company, Security & Closing Date, not in this file.

Upgrading to a new version

  1. Make a backup of the database (Backup & Restore, phpMyAdmin Export, or tools/export_sql.php), and a copy of the program folder including storage/ and config/config.php.
  2. Copy the new program files over the old ones. Keep your config/config.php.
  3. In phpMyAdmin (Import) or Navicat (Execute SQL File), select your company database and run the files in database/migrations/ that you have not run yet, oldest first.
  4. Log in. If a file is still missing, the program stops after log-in with the page Database update needed, which names the files still to run, in order.

GSMaRT Construction System 1.0 is delivered with the complete database and needs no upgrade file. Later versions put theirs in database/migrations/, each written for MySQL 5.5, keeping every record and safe to run twice.

Important

Never import database/gsmart_construction_sample.sql or database/gsmart_construction_clean.sql over a database that is in use. Both drop and recreate every table: all your records would be replaced by the sample data. Upgrade only with the files in database/migrations/.

  • With several companies, every company database needs the update. The Super Admin runs them with Companies > Database updates on the platform pages, or you run php tools/platform.php update-all.
  • After upgrading from a version with the older average costing, you may run Rebuild data once in Utilities: Verify & Rebuild Data to recost past sales. It is optional: new transactions use the current costing at once. Make a backup first, because the cost of past sales may change.

Command-line tools

Run them from the program folder, for example php tools/install.php. They refuse to run from a browser.

  • Windows (Task Scheduler): create a daily task with the program C:\xampp\php\php.exe and the argument C:\xampp\htdocs\gsmart-construction\tools\send_scheduled_reports.php.

Folder What it holds

Backups

  • Inside the program: Backup & Restore writes the whole company database to one file in storage/backups, lists and downloads the backups, and restores one. A restore always makes a fresh backup of the current data first, and asks you to type RESTORE.
  • From the command line: php tools/export_sql.php --out=FILE.
  • phpMyAdmin: Export.
  • With several companies, back up the platform database (accounting_system_master, or the name set in the config) together with the company databases.

Whichever way you use, keep a copy away from the server.

  • assets/vendor/bootstrap/css/bootstrap.min.css
  • assets/vendor/bootstrap/js/bootstrap.bundle.min.js
  • assets/vendor/bootstrap-icons/font/bootstrap-icons.min.css
  • assets/vendor/bootstrap-icons/font/fonts/bootstrap-icons.woff2
  • assets/vendor/bootstrap-icons/font/fonts/bootstrap-icons.woff

The program uses the local copies automatically when they are there.

Web server notes

  • Apache: the included .htaccess files block the program's internal folders and .sql and .md files.
  • nginx: deny the folders app, config, database, modules, storage, tools and views, hidden files, and .sql and .md files, and pass .php files to PHP-FPM. The README has a sample configuration.
  • Printing receipts to a Bluetooth roll printer needs the page to be opened on an https:// address or on http://localhost/.

Checklist before going live

  1. Import accounting_system_clean.sql, or run php tools/install.php without --demo.
  2. Change the admin password, and change or deactivate the demo users you do not need.
  3. Create a MySQL user just for this program (instead of root) and put it in config/config.php.
  4. Use HTTPS. The session cookie becomes secure automatically.
  5. Keep APP_DEBUG off.
  6. Enter the company details, password rules and session timeout in Company, Security & Closing Date.
  7. Check the Chart of Accounts and Module Default Accounts, the tax codes, terms, sites, POS terminals and payment methods.
  8. Review the roles, page access and special permissions (Appendix B), and give every user their own account.
  9. Enter the opening balances and opening stock, then set the closing date to the day before you start.
  10. Make a backup before each month-end close and keep a copy off the server.
  11. With several companies: keep the Super Admin's password safe (a second Super Admin is a good idea), check that each company has a Company Owner, and back up the platform database with the company databases.
